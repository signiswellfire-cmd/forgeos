# ForgeOS Code of Conduct

## Purpose

ForgeOS is an engineering project built around professionalism, intellectual honesty, and long-term collaboration.

This Code of Conduct establishes the expected behavior for everyone participating in the project, whether contributing code, documentation, architectural proposals, reviews, discussions, or organizational knowledge.

The objective is to create an engineering environment where ideas are challenged rigorously while people are treated respectfully.

---

# Core Values

Every participant is expected to uphold the following values.

## Professionalism

Conduct discussions as professional engineering conversations.

Critique ideas rather than individuals.

Support technical arguments with reasoning and evidence whenever possible.

---

## Intellectual Honesty

Engineering decisions should be based on facts, measurable trade-offs, and long-term maintainability.

If evidence contradicts a previous decision, contributors are encouraged to challenge the decision respectfully and propose a better alternative.

Being correct is more valuable than being consistent.

---

## Respect

Treat every contributor with respect regardless of:

* Experience
* Background
* Nationality
* Language
* Employer
* Technical preferences

Respect does not require agreement.

Disagreement is expected.

Disrespect is not.

---

## Knowledge First

ForgeOS values permanent engineering knowledge over temporary conversations.

When important architectural decisions are reached, they should be documented within the repository rather than remaining in chat messages or personal notes.

The goal is to strengthen the organization rather than individuals.

---

## Engineering Excellence

Contributors are encouraged to:

* Reduce unnecessary complexity.
* Improve architectural consistency.
* Preserve engineering quality.
* Protect maintainability.
* Think beyond immediate implementation.

Short-term convenience should never compromise long-term engineering health.

---

# Expected Behavior

Participants are expected to:

* Communicate respectfully.
* Assume good intent.
* Explain technical reasoning.
* Welcome constructive criticism.
* Document significant decisions.
* Help improve the project beyond their own contributions.
* Prioritize long-term project success over personal preferences.

---

# Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are not acceptable:

* Personal attacks.
* Harassment or discrimination.
* Deliberate misinformation.
* Hostile or abusive language.
* Repeated disregard for established engineering standards.
* Intentionally introducing undocumented architectural changes.
* Knowingly misrepresenting technical facts.

---

# Architectural Discussions

ForgeOS encourages challenging architectural assumptions.

Healthy architectural discussion includes:

* Identifying weaknesses.
* Proposing alternatives.
* Explaining trade-offs.
* Supporting recommendations with evidence.

Engineering disagreement is encouraged.

Personal conflict is not.

---

# Decision Authority

ForgeOS separates technical authority from product authority.

## Founder Responsibilities

The Founder owns:

* Product vision
* Business strategy
* Product positioning
* Licensing decisions
* Final architectural approval

## Engineering Responsibilities

Engineering contributors own:

* Technical implementation
* Engineering documentation
* Architectural proposals
* Technical reviews
* Standards
* Engineering quality

This separation reduces ambiguity while preserving clear accountability.

---

# Enforcement

Project maintainers may request contributors to revise behavior that conflicts with this Code of Conduct.

Repeated or severe violations may result in removal of contribution privileges at the discretion of the project maintainers.

---

# Living Document

This Code of Conduct is a living engineering document.

It should evolve as ForgeOS grows while preserving its fundamental principles:

* Professionalism
* Respect
* Intellectual honesty
* Engineering excellence
* Knowledge preservation

All revisions should improve the project's ability to sustain a healthy engineering organization over the long term.
