# Purpose

This Technical Design Specification defines the ForgeOS Domain Model.

It specifies the business architecture that underpins ForgeOS Core, including domain decomposition, aggregate boundaries, entity ownership, value objects, repository contracts, persistence ownership, and domain event relationships.

This document is implementation-oriented.

---

# Ubiquitous Language

ForgeOS adopts a single **Ubiquitous Language** shared by architects, engineers, AI implementation agents, reviewers, and documentation.

The terms defined in this section are normative throughout the repository.

Where a term defined here conflicts with informal usage, the definition in this section takes precedence.

---

## Core Business Terms

| Term | Canonical Meaning |
|------|-------------------|
| Organization | The persistent business identity that owns missions, governance, workforce, knowledge, and organizational capabilities. |
| Mission | A business objective pursued by the Organization. Missions define *why* work is performed. |
| Process | A repeatable workflow describing *how* work is executed to accomplish a Mission. |
| Knowledge | Validated organizational understanding that has been promoted for long-term organizational use. |
| Memory | Historical organizational experience preserved for traceability and institutional learning. |
| Workforce | The organizational capability composed of professionals, competencies, teams, and skills. |

## Architectural Terms

| Term | Canonical Meaning |
|------|-------------------|
| Bounded Context | A business capability with exclusive ownership of its language, business rules, and aggregates. |
| Aggregate | A transactional consistency boundary that protects business invariants. |

| Repository | The domain contract responsible for persisting one aggregate root. |

# Domain Modeling Principles

The ForgeOS domain model is governed by the following principles.

1. Every aggregate has one architectural owner.
2. Every entity belongs to exactly one aggregate.
3. Value Objects are immutable.
4. Business invariants are enforced within aggregate boundaries.
5. Repository ownership follows aggregate ownership.
6. Cross-domain interaction occurs through identifiers and domain events.
7. Aggregate boundaries are consistency boundaries.
8. Persistence follows architectural ownership.
9. No aggregate directly modifies another aggregate.
10. Business rules reside exclusively within the Domain Layer.

These principles are mandatory.

# Aggregate Design Principles

Aggregates define transactional consistency boundaries.

Every aggregate:

- owns its entities;
- protects its invariants;
- exposes behavior rather than mutable state;
- publishes domain events;
- persists through one repository interface.

Aggregates shall never expose internal entities for external mutation.

---

# Identity Strategy

ForgeOS distinguishes between:

## Aggregate Identity

Uniquely identifies an aggregate instance.

Characteristics:

- immutable;
- globally unique within the repository;
- stable throughout lifecycle.

---

## Entity Identity

Uniquely identifies an entity within its owning aggregate.

Entity identity has no meaning outside the aggregate boundary.

---

## Value Objects

Value Objects are identified solely by value.

Characteristics:

- immutable;
- equality by value;
- no independent lifecycle;
- no repository.

---

# Aggregate Lifecycle

Every aggregate follows the same conceptual lifecycle.

```text
Created
    ↓
Initialized
    ↓
Active
    ↓
Modified
    ↓
Archived
```

Individual aggregates may refine this lifecycle but shall not violate the architectural model.

---

# Repository Contracts

Every aggregate owns one repository interface.

Repository interfaces belong to the Domain.

Repository implementations belong to Infrastructure.

Repository contracts define:

- retrieval;
- persistence;
- existence checks;
- optimistic concurrency;
- archival.

Repository contracts shall not expose persistence technology.

---

# Domain Services

Domain Services exist only when business behavior cannot naturally belong to one aggregate.

Characteristics:

- stateless;
- business-oriented;
- domain-owned;
- technology-independent.

Application orchestration remains outside Domain Services.

---

# Persistence Ownership

Persistence ownership follows aggregate ownership.

Rules:

- every aggregate has one repository;
- repositories persist one aggregate root;
- shared persistence ownership is prohibited;
- read models do not own business data.

---

# Architectural Invariants

The following constraints shall always remain true.

- Aggregate ownership is singular.
- Entity ownership is singular.
- Value Objects remain immutable.
- Cross-aggregate updates occur through orchestration.
- Domain entities remain persistence-agnostic.
- Business rules remain inside aggregates or domain services.

Violation requires architectural review.

# Cross-Context Relationships

Bounded Contexts collaborate through explicit architectural contracts.

No bounded context shall directly modify the internal state of another bounded context.

Cross-context collaboration occurs through:

- published domain events;
- application service orchestration;
- immutable identifiers;
- repository interfaces owned by the originating context.

This preserves aggregate consistency while enabling coordinated organizational behavior.

# Consistency Model

ForgeOS adopts explicit consistency boundaries.

## Within an Aggregate

Consistency is immediate.

The aggregate guarantees all business invariants before completion of the transaction.

---

## Across Aggregates

Consistency is coordinated.

Cross-aggregate operations are orchestrated through the Application Layer.

Business domains shall not coordinate transactions directly.

---

## Across Bounded Contexts

Consistency is eventually achieved through:

- published domain events;
- application orchestration;
- explicit business workflows.

No bounded context may directly persist another bounded context's aggregate.

# Identity Model

| Identity Type | Scope |
|--------------|-------|
| Aggregate Identity | Globally unique within the repository |
| Entity Identity | Unique within an aggregate |
| Value Object | Identified solely by value |

Identity ownership follows aggregate ownership.

# Persistence Model Summary

Persistence responsibilities are derived from architectural ownership.

The following rules are normative.

- Every aggregate owns one repository interface.
- Infrastructure implements repository contracts.
- Repository implementations remain replaceable.
- Business domains remain persistence-agnostic.
- Read models never become aggregate owners.

---

# Domain Architectural Invariants

The following invariants apply to the complete Domain Model.

1. Every aggregate has exactly one architectural owner.
2. Every entity belongs to exactly one aggregate.
3. Every repository persists exactly one aggregate root.
4. Domain events originate from one bounded context.
5. Value Objects are immutable.
6. Aggregate boundaries define transactional consistency.
7. Business rules remain inside bounded contexts.
8. Persistence ownership follows architectural ownership.
9. Cross-context communication occurs only through approved contracts.
10. Architectural ownership shall not be inferred from implementation.

These invariants define the permanent business architecture of ForgeOS.
