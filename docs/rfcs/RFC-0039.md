# RFC-0039 — Knowledge Lifecycle Engine

**RFC Number:** RFC-0039

**Title:** Knowledge Lifecycle Engine

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Knowledge Lifecycle Engine (KLE), the subsystem responsible for governing the complete lifecycle of organizational knowledge within ForgeOS.

The Knowledge Lifecycle Engine ensures that every organizational knowledge asset progresses through a controlled lifecycle from creation to archival while maintaining governance, traceability, version history, and organizational integrity.

Knowledge is treated as a living organizational asset rather than static documentation.

---

# Motivation

Organizations continuously create knowledge through:

* Mission execution;
* Executive Meetings;
* architectural design;
* engineering implementation;
* production operations;
* customer engagement;
* organizational learning.

Without lifecycle governance, knowledge gradually becomes:

* duplicated;
* outdated;
* contradictory;
* difficult to trust;
* expensive to maintain.

ForgeOS requires systematic lifecycle management.

---

# Problem Statement

Traditional documentation systems focus primarily on storing documents.

They rarely manage:

* knowledge evolution;
* ownership;
* governance;
* quality;
* retirement;
* historical lineage.

As organizations mature, unmanaged knowledge becomes organizational debt.

ForgeOS therefore introduces lifecycle governance as a first-class capability.

---

# Goals

The Knowledge Lifecycle Engine shall:

* govern knowledge evolution;
* maintain organizational trust;
* preserve historical lineage;
* coordinate review cycles;
* reduce knowledge debt;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

The Knowledge Lifecycle Engine does not define:

* document editors;
* storage technologies;
* version control systems;
* content management systems;
* collaboration tools.

Those concerns belong to implementation specifications.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the Knowledge Lifecycle Engine as the authoritative subsystem governing organizational knowledge evolution.

Every governed Knowledge Object shall participate in a defined lifecycle.

Lifecycle state becomes mandatory organizational metadata.

---

# Lifecycle Principles

## Knowledge is Organizational

Knowledge belongs to the Organization rather than individual contributors.

---

## Knowledge Evolves

Organizational knowledge continuously improves through validated experience.

Stagnation is treated as organizational risk.

---

## Governance Before Publication

Knowledge shall not become authoritative without appropriate organizational review.

---

## Historical Preservation

Historical knowledge remains available for traceability even after retirement.

Knowledge should evolve rather than disappear.

---

## Single Source of Truth

Every organizational concept shall have exactly one authoritative representation.

Superseded knowledge shall explicitly reference its replacement.

---

# Knowledge Lifecycle

Every governed Knowledge Object progresses through the following lifecycle.

## Proposed

Knowledge candidate identified.

Initial organizational value recognized.

---

## Draft

Knowledge actively developed.

Not yet authoritative.

---

## Review

Technical, organizational, or executive validation occurs.

Governance requirements depend on knowledge category.

---

## Approved

Knowledge becomes organizationally authoritative.

Approved knowledge may be referenced by other organizational assets.

---

## Active

Knowledge is actively used throughout the Organization.

Lifecycle monitoring continues.

---

## Improving

Validated improvements are incorporated while preserving historical lineage.

Version evolution remains traceable.

---

## Deprecated

Knowledge remains available but should no longer be used for new organizational work.

Replacement guidance should be provided.

---

## Archived

Knowledge retained exclusively for historical reference.

Archived knowledge is excluded from active recommendations unless historical context is explicitly requested.

---

# Lifecycle Events

Representative lifecycle events include:

* creation;
* ownership transfer;
* review completion;
* approval;
* supersession;
* retirement;
* restoration;
* archival.

Lifecycle events become organizational knowledge.

---

# Knowledge Ownership

Every governed Knowledge Object shall define:

* organizational owner;
* steward;
* approval authority;
* review responsibility.

Ownership is organizational rather than personal.

---

# Version Management

Each lifecycle transition records:

* version identifier;
* effective date;
* authorizing decision;
* rationale;
* superseded versions;
* related knowledge.

Version history shall remain immutable.

---

# Review Policy

Knowledge review frequency depends upon:

* governance criticality;
* organizational usage;
* engineering impact;
* regulatory obligations;
* strategic importance.

Review schedules should be adaptive rather than fixed.

---

# Knowledge Quality

Quality evaluation considers:

* correctness;
* completeness;
* consistency;
* traceability;
* authority;
* reuse;
* organizational relevance.

Quality indicators contribute to Organization Health.

---

# Knowledge Retirement

Knowledge retirement occurs when:

* superseded;
* obsolete;
* organizationally irrelevant;
* merged;
* deprecated.

Retirement shall preserve:

* historical references;
* dependency relationships;
* organizational reasoning.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Promotion

Knowledge Promotion identifies reusable organizational learning.

The Knowledge Lifecycle Engine governs that knowledge after promotion.

Promotion creates.

Lifecycle governs.

---

# Relationship to Engineering Memory

Engineering Memory references active knowledge throughout its lifecycle.

Lifecycle transitions automatically influence engineering recommendations.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Query Engine

The Knowledge Query Engine retrieves only knowledge appropriate to lifecycle state.

Deprecated and archived knowledge require explicit retrieval intent.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Recommendation Engine

Recommendations prioritize Active knowledge.

Deprecated knowledge may only be recommended when historical understanding is required.

---

# Relationship to Organization Health

Organization Health evaluates:

* lifecycle completeness;
* review compliance;
* knowledge freshness;
* ownership coverage;
* quality trends.

Lifecycle maturity contributes directly to organizational maturity.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Lifecycle state becomes a first-class property of every Knowledge Graph node.

Relationships include:

* supersedes;
* superseded_by;
* archived_from;
* promoted_from;
* reviewed_by;
* owned_by.

These relationships preserve organizational evolution.

---

# Governance

Lifecycle transitions require documented authorization.

Approval requirements vary by knowledge category but always preserve:

* traceability;
* accountability;
* historical integrity.

Unauthorized lifecycle transitions are prohibited.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Document Versioning Only

Rejected because version history alone cannot govern organizational knowledge.

---

## Wiki-Based Governance

Rejected because wiki systems typically lack lifecycle enforcement and organizational authority modeling.

---

## Manual Review Processes

Rejected because manual governance alone does not scale to enterprise organizational knowledge.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* trustworthy organizational knowledge;
* reduced knowledge debt;
* improved governance;
* stronger traceability;
* sustainable knowledge evolution;
* higher organizational confidence.

Trade-offs include:

* governance effort;
* metadata management;
* lifecycle administration.

These trade-offs are accepted because they substantially improve long-term organizational intelligence.
