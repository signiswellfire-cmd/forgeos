# RFC-0022 — Process Engine

**RFC Number:** RFC-0022

**Title:** Process Engine

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Process Engine, the subsystem responsible for executing, governing, monitoring, and evolving organizational processes within ForgeOS.

Where the Mission Engine manages **what** the Organization intends to accomplish, the Process Engine governs **how** organizational work is executed consistently.

The Process Engine transforms organizational standards into repeatable operational behavior.

---

# Motivation

Successful engineering organizations depend upon repeatable processes rather than individual habits.

Examples include:

* architectural review,
* code review,
* testing,
* incident response,
* release management,
* documentation review,
* knowledge promotion.

Traditional workflow engines execute predefined steps but rarely understand organizational governance or capability development.

ForgeOS therefore models processes as organizational assets rather than automation scripts.

---

# Problem Statement

Without standardized organizational processes:

* execution quality varies;
* governance becomes inconsistent;
* engineering knowledge is difficult to reuse;
* organizational capability stagnates;
* automation becomes fragmented.

Organizations require a permanent process model independent of implementation technologies.

---

# Goals

The Process Engine shall:

* execute organizational processes;
* enforce governance;
* integrate with Missions;
* preserve organizational standards;
* enable process evolution;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

The Process Engine does not define:

* BPMN syntax;
* workflow execution software;
* orchestration frameworks;
* scheduling infrastructure;
* UI implementation.

Implementation details belong to future Technical Design Specifications.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the Process Engine as the authoritative execution framework for all repeatable organizational processes.

Processes become governed organizational assets that may evolve independently from implementation technologies.

---

# Process Definition

A Process represents a repeatable organizational behavior that transforms defined inputs into governed outputs.

Each Process contains:

* identifier;
* name;
* purpose;
* scope;
* triggering conditions;
* participating Professionals;
* required Capabilities;
* activities;
* decision points;
* validation rules;
* outputs;
* lifecycle state;
* version history.

---

# Process Lifecycle

Every Process progresses through:

## Proposed

Initial organizational need identified.

---

## Designed

Activities, governance, and expected outcomes defined.

---

## Approved

Process validated through organizational governance.

---

## Active

Process available for organizational execution.

---

## Improved

Process updated through organizational learning.

Historical versions remain traceable.

---

## Deprecated

Process scheduled for replacement.

---

## Archived

Historical reference retained.

---

# Process Structure

Every Process contains the following conceptual stages.

```text
Trigger
    ↓
Preparation
    ↓
Execution
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Completion
    ↓
Knowledge Capture
```

Individual implementations may expand these stages while preserving their intent.

---

# Trigger Model

Processes may begin because of:

* Mission creation;
* Executive decision;
* organizational event;
* operational incident;
* scheduled governance;
* user request;
* system event.

Triggers initiate process execution.

They do not bypass governance.

---

# Activities

Activities represent discrete units of organizational work.

Each activity should define:

* objective;
* owner;
* prerequisites;
* expected outputs;
* completion criteria.

Activities remain subordinate to the Process.

---

# Decision Points

Processes may contain decision points.

Decision points:

* evaluate organizational conditions;
* determine execution paths;
* invoke governance;
* preserve explainability.

Decision logic should remain explicit and reviewable.

---

# Validation

Every governed Process defines validation requirements.

Validation may include:

* engineering review;
* quality assurance;
* compliance verification;
* architectural approval;
* security review.

Validation ensures organizational consistency.

---

# Process Ownership

Every Process has:

* one organizational owner;
* one or more stewards;
* participating Professionals;
* defined governance authority.

Ownership remains organizational rather than personal.

---

# Process Evolution

Processes improve through:

* Experience Capture;
* Mission outcomes;
* Executive Meetings;
* promoted Knowledge;
* Capability improvements.

Process changes should preserve historical traceability.

---

# Relationship to Missions

Processes support Mission execution.

Missions invoke Processes.

Processes remain reusable across many Missions.

This separation prevents duplication of organizational behavior.

---

# Relationship to Blueprints

Blueprints may instantiate predefined Processes.

Organizations should reuse existing Processes whenever possible before creating new ones.

---

# Relationship to Engineering Standards

Engineering Standards constrain Process behavior.

Processes implement standards.

They do not redefine them.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Processes become graph nodes connected to:

* Missions;
* Professionals;
* Standards;
* Capabilities;
* Decisions;
* Blueprints;
* Knowledge Objects.

This enables explainable organizational execution.

---

# Governance

Process modification requires:

* documented rationale;
* impact assessment;
* organizational review;
* version management.

Process evolution should be deliberate.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Workflow Automation Platforms

Rejected because workflow engines generally lack organizational knowledge, governance, and capability modeling.

---

## Static Documentation

Rejected because documentation alone cannot govern execution.

---

## AI-Orchestrated Workflows

Rejected because AI execution should operate within organizational processes rather than define them.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* repeatable organizational execution;
* stronger governance;
* improved engineering consistency;
* reusable organizational behavior;
* measurable process maturity.

Trade-offs include:

* governance overhead;
* lifecycle management;
* process stewardship.

These trade-offs are accepted because they improve long-term organizational capability.
