# RFC-0042 — Knowledge Federation

**RFC Number:** RFC-0042

**Title:** Knowledge Federation

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Knowledge Federation architecture for ForgeOS.

Knowledge Federation enables multiple independent Organizations to securely discover, exchange, reference, and collaborate on governed knowledge while preserving organizational ownership, sovereignty, lifecycle governance, and traceability.

Rather than centralizing all organizational knowledge into a single repository, ForgeOS establishes a federated knowledge architecture where each Organization remains authoritative for its own knowledge while participating in a governed knowledge ecosystem.

---

# Purpose

The Knowledge Federation architecture enables Organizations to:

* reuse proven organizational knowledge;
* collaborate without merging repositories;
* preserve organizational ownership;
* reduce duplicated engineering effort;
* accelerate organizational learning;
* establish trusted knowledge networks.

Knowledge sharing becomes intentional, governed, and traceable.

---

# Scope

This RFC defines:

* federation architecture;
* federation principles;
* knowledge ownership;
* federation lifecycle;
* federation governance;
* federation trust model.

Implementation protocols, transport mechanisms, and synchronization technologies are intentionally excluded.

---

# Context

ForgeOS already defines:

* Knowledge Model;
* Knowledge Graph;
* Knowledge Lifecycle;
* Knowledge Query Engine;
* Organizational Search;
* Organization Memory.

These systems govern knowledge within a single Organization.

Knowledge Federation extends these capabilities across organizational boundaries.

---

# Problem Statement

Organizations frequently possess valuable knowledge that could benefit other Organizations.

Traditional approaches often require:

* repository duplication;
* document exports;
* manual synchronization;
* shared file systems;
* centralized ownership.

These approaches weaken governance and create inconsistent organizational knowledge.

ForgeOS requires a federated model.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces Knowledge Federation as the authoritative architecture for cross-organization knowledge sharing.

Every participating Organization retains complete authority over its own governed knowledge.

Federation enables discovery and controlled reuse without transferring ownership.

---

# Federation Principles

## Sovereign Ownership

Each Organization owns its knowledge.

Ownership never transfers through federation.

---

## Federated Discovery

Organizations may discover external knowledge without importing entire repositories.

Discovery precedes acquisition.

---

## Governance Preservation

Federation shall never bypass lifecycle governance, approval processes, or organizational policies.

---

## Explicit Trust

Knowledge exchange occurs only through explicitly established federation relationships.

Trust is intentional.

---

## Explainable Origin

Every federated knowledge asset shall preserve:

* originating Organization;
* authoritative owner;
* lifecycle state;
* version;
* provenance.

Origin shall remain permanently visible.

---

# Federation Architecture

Knowledge Federation consists of:

## Federation Participants

Independent ForgeOS Organizations.

---

## Federation Links

Governed trust relationships between Organizations.

---

## Federation Registry

Catalog of participating Organizations and federation metadata.

---

## Federation Policies

Rules controlling discovery, access, synchronization, and reuse.

---

## Federation Exchange

Controlled knowledge sharing between trusted participants.

---

# Federated Knowledge Types

Representative federated assets include:

* RFCs;
* Engineering Standards;
* Blueprints;
* Knowledge Objects;
* Capability Definitions;
* Skill Libraries;
* Competency Frameworks;
* Process Templates;
* Architecture Patterns;
* Improvement Practices.

Organizations may define additional governed knowledge types.

---

# Federation Lifecycle

Federated knowledge progresses through:

1. Publication
2. Discovery
3. Eligibility Evaluation
4. Access Authorization
5. Federation
6. Local Referencing
7. Synchronization
8. Retirement

Federation never alters ownership.

---

# Federation Trust Model

Federation relationships define:

* participating Organizations;
* permitted knowledge categories;
* synchronization policies;
* access permissions;
* review obligations;
* termination conditions.

Trust relationships require executive approval.

---

# Knowledge Referencing

Organizations should reference external knowledge whenever practical.

Local duplication should occur only when organizational independence requires it.

Federated references preserve synchronization opportunities.

---

# Synchronization

Knowledge synchronization should preserve:

* lifecycle state;
* version lineage;
* supersession history;
* authoritative ownership;
* federation metadata.

Synchronization shall never overwrite local organizational decisions.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Query Engine

The Knowledge Query Engine may retrieve federated knowledge when federation policies permit.

Results shall distinguish:

* local authoritative knowledge;
* federated authoritative knowledge.

This distinction shall remain explicit.

---

# Relationship to Organizational Search

The Organizational Search Engine may discover federated organizational assets.

Discovery does not imply access.

Access remains governed by federation policy.

---

# Relationship to Organization Memory

Organization Memory records:

* federation establishment;
* federation usage;
* imported references;
* organizational impact.

Federation history becomes institutional memory.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Graph

Knowledge Federation extends the Knowledge Graph across organizational boundaries.

Cross-organization relationships become governed graph edges while preserving organizational sovereignty.

---

# Governance

Knowledge Federation requires:

* executive sponsorship;
* documented trust relationships;
* lifecycle governance;
* provenance preservation;
* federation policy compliance.

Unauthorized federation is prohibited.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Central Knowledge Repository

Rejected because centralized ownership weakens organizational sovereignty.

---

## Repository Mirroring

Rejected because mirroring creates duplicate authority and synchronization challenges.

---

## Manual Knowledge Exchange

Rejected because manual processes do not scale across multiple Organizations.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* secure organizational collaboration;
* improved Blueprint reuse;
* stronger organizational learning;
* preserved governance;
* reduced duplicated effort;
* scalable knowledge ecosystems.

Trade-offs include:

* federation governance;
* trust management;
* synchronization complexity.

These trade-offs are accepted because they enable governed collaboration without sacrificing organizational autonomy.
