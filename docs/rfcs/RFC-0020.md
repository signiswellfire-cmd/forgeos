# RFC-0020 — Engineering Standards Framework

**RFC Number:** RFC-0020

**Title:** Engineering Standards Framework

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC establishes the Engineering Standards Framework for ForgeOS.

Engineering Standards define the mandatory rules governing how software, documentation, architecture, testing, security, operations, and organizational knowledge are produced within ForgeOS.

Unlike implementation guides, Engineering Standards establish organizational expectations. They ensure that engineering quality is repeatable, measurable, auditable, and independent of individual contributors.

This RFC defines the framework that future engineering standards must follow.

---

# Motivation

Engineering excellence cannot depend upon individual discipline.

Without explicit standards:

* engineering quality varies between contributors;
* documentation becomes inconsistent;
* architectural integrity erodes;
* onboarding becomes expensive;
* technical debt accumulates;
* governance weakens.

ForgeOS therefore treats standards as first-class organizational assets.

---

# Problem Statement

Organizations frequently embed engineering expectations inside:

* tribal knowledge,
* code review comments,
* team habits,
* undocumented conventions.

These approaches do not scale.

Engineering expectations must instead become permanent organizational knowledge.

---

# Goals

The Engineering Standards Framework shall:

* establish a common engineering baseline;
* support organizational governance;
* improve consistency;
* enable measurable quality;
* reduce ambiguity;
* remain technology independent.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define individual engineering standards.

Specific standards such as coding conventions or testing policies are defined separately under the `/docs/standards` directory.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS adopts a formal Engineering Standards Framework.

Every engineering standard shall be:

* versioned;
* reviewable;
* traceable;
* independently authoritative;
* governed through organizational approval.

Standards become permanent organizational knowledge after approval.

---

# Standard Categories

The initial standards library consists of the following categories.

## Architecture Standards

Govern architectural boundaries, modularity, dependency direction, and long-term system evolution.

---

## Documentation Standards

Define documentation structure, writing quality, versioning, traceability, and maintenance expectations.

---

## Coding Standards

Define language-specific implementation practices.

Coding standards may differ between languages while remaining aligned with architectural principles.

---

## Testing Standards

Govern unit testing, integration testing, system testing, regression testing, and quality verification.

---

## Security Standards

Define secure engineering practices including authentication, authorization, data protection, secrets management, auditing, and vulnerability management.

---

## Git Standards

Define repository organization, branching strategy, commit conventions, pull request expectations, and release tagging.

---

## API Standards

Govern public interfaces, versioning, compatibility, error handling, and API lifecycle management.

---

## Operational Standards

Define deployment, monitoring, backup, disaster recovery, observability, and operational excellence.

---

## Knowledge Standards

Govern Knowledge Objects, Blueprint creation, Experience Capture, Executive Memory, and Knowledge Promotion.

---

# Standard Lifecycle

Every engineering standard progresses through the following lifecycle.

## Draft

Initial proposal.

---

## Review

Technical validation.

---

## Approved

Authoritative organizational standard.

---

## Active

Mandatory organizational guidance.

---

## Deprecated

Scheduled for replacement.

---

## Archived

Retained for historical reference.

---

# Standard Structure

Every Engineering Standard shall contain:

* purpose;
* scope;
* applicability;
* normative requirements;
* rationale;
* examples;
* exceptions;
* compliance expectations;
* revision history;
* references.

Standards should explain why requirements exist, not merely state rules.

---

# Compliance

Compliance with Engineering Standards is expected for all permanent organizational artifacts.

Exceptions require documented justification and appropriate organizational approval.

Non-compliance should be treated as organizational debt.

---

# Governance

Engineering Standards are governed through the ForgeOS architectural process.

Changes require:

* documented proposal;
* technical review;
* impact assessment;
* approval authority;
* version update.

Breaking changes should be introduced deliberately and remain traceable.

---

# Relationship to RFCs

RFCs define architectural decisions.

Engineering Standards define how those decisions are consistently implemented.

An RFC may reference one or more Engineering Standards.

Engineering Standards should not redefine architectural decisions.

---

# Relationship to TDS

Technical Design Specifications implement RFCs while conforming to Engineering Standards.

Engineering Standards therefore provide implementation constraints rather than implementation details.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Engineering Standards are permanent Knowledge Objects.

They participate in graph relationships including:

* governs,
* references,
* supersedes,
* validates,
* implements,
* constrains.

These relationships improve organizational reasoning and impact analysis.

---

# Organizational Principles

Engineering Standards follow these principles.

## Consistency Over Preference

Organizational consistency takes precedence over personal coding style.

---

## Explainability Over Complexity

Standards should remain understandable and teachable.

---

## Long-Term Maintainability

Standards should optimize for decades of organizational evolution.

---

## Technology Independence

Where practical, standards should emphasize engineering principles rather than specific tools.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Standards evolve through validated organizational learning rather than isolated opinion.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Team Conventions

Rejected because conventions vary between contributors and are rarely governed.

---

## Tool-Enforced Rules Only

Rejected because automated tooling cannot express architectural reasoning or organizational principles.

---

## Language-Specific Standards Only

Rejected because ForgeOS requires organization-wide engineering consistency beyond programming languages.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* improved engineering consistency;
* stronger governance;
* repeatable engineering quality;
* simplified onboarding;
* reduced technical debt;
* explainable engineering practices.

Trade-offs include:

* governance overhead;
* standard maintenance;
* periodic review requirements.

These trade-offs are accepted because they strengthen long-term organizational capability.
