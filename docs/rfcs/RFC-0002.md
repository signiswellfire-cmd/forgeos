# RFC-0002 — Knowledge Model

**RFC Number:** RFC-0002

**Title:** Knowledge Model

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the ForgeOS Knowledge Model.

Knowledge is the most important asset managed by ForgeOS. Unlike source code, conversations, or AI-generated responses, knowledge is intended to become a permanent organizational capability that accumulates throughout the lifetime of an organization.

This RFC specifies how knowledge is represented, classified, owned, validated, promoted, and evolved.

---

# Motivation

Software organizations repeatedly lose valuable experience because engineering knowledge exists primarily in:

* conversations,
* chat history,
* issue trackers,
* individual contributors,
* temporary AI sessions.

ForgeOS treats knowledge as a first-class domain object instead of incidental documentation.

Every completed mission should strengthen organizational capability through new knowledge.

---

# Problem Statement

Without an explicit knowledge model:

* architectural reasoning disappears,
* successful practices are forgotten,
* mistakes are repeated,
* onboarding becomes increasingly expensive,
* organizational capability depends upon individuals.

Knowledge must therefore be represented independently from implementation artifacts.

---

# Goals

The Knowledge Model shall:

* represent permanent engineering knowledge;
* support organizational learning;
* preserve engineering rationale;
* enable traceability;
* support validation and promotion;
* remain independent from implementation technology.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* storage implementation;
* graph persistence;
* search algorithms;
* AI retrieval;
* embedding strategies;
* database schemas.

These concerns belong to future TDS and TDR documents.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces **Knowledge Objects** as the canonical representation of permanent organizational knowledge.

Every reusable engineering insight should eventually become one or more Knowledge Objects.

Knowledge is never owned by an individual contributor.

Knowledge belongs to the Organization.

---

# Knowledge Object

A Knowledge Object is the smallest permanent unit of organizational knowledge.

Every Knowledge Object should be:

* uniquely identifiable;
* versioned;
* reviewable;
* explainable;
* reusable;
* independently referenceable.

Knowledge Objects should remain meaningful even when separated from their originating mission.

---

# Knowledge Categories

Knowledge may exist in multiple categories.

## Architectural Knowledge

Examples:

* architectural decisions,
* system boundaries,
* integration patterns.

---

## Organizational Knowledge

Examples:

* governance,
* responsibilities,
* operating procedures,
* standards.

---

## Domain Knowledge

Examples:

* business rules,
* domain terminology,
* workflows,
* policies.

---

## Technical Knowledge

Examples:

* implementation patterns,
* reusable algorithms,
* infrastructure guidance.

---

## Operational Knowledge

Examples:

* deployment procedures,
* maintenance guidance,
* incident handling,
* monitoring strategies.

---

## Experience Knowledge

Examples:

* lessons learned,
* retrospective outcomes,
* engineering recommendations,
* validated practices.

---

# Knowledge Lifecycle

Knowledge progresses through the following lifecycle.

## Draft

Initial engineering understanding.

Knowledge has not yet been validated.

---

## Review

Knowledge is undergoing technical review.

Review focuses on correctness, completeness, and organizational value.

---

## Approved

Knowledge becomes authoritative.

Approved Knowledge Objects become part of permanent organizational memory.

---

## Deprecated

Knowledge is retained for historical reference but should no longer guide engineering decisions.

---

## Archived

Knowledge remains preserved but is no longer expected to evolve.

---

# Knowledge Ownership

Knowledge belongs to the Organization.

Professionals may create, review, refine, or promote Knowledge Objects, but ownership remains institutional.

This prevents organizational capability from becoming dependent upon specific contributors.

---

# Knowledge Promotion

Temporary information becomes permanent only after validation.

Typical promotion flow:

1. Mission execution.
2. Artifact creation.
3. Engineering review.
4. Validation.
5. Knowledge promotion.
6. Organizational reuse.

Promotion is intentional.

Not every artifact becomes permanent knowledge.

---

# Knowledge Relationships

Knowledge Objects may reference one another.

Typical relationships include:

* derives from,
* supersedes,
* depends on,
* validates,
* elaborates,
* replaces,
* references.

Relationship semantics are defined in RFC-0003.

---

# Knowledge Quality

Every approved Knowledge Object should satisfy the following attributes.

## Correct

Technically accurate.

---

## Explainable

Includes rationale and context.

---

## Reusable

Applicable beyond a single implementation.

---

## Traceable

References its origin and related decisions.

---

## Maintainable

Can evolve without invalidating organizational history.

---

# Governance

Knowledge promotion should require validation.

Validation authority depends on knowledge type.

Examples:

* engineering standards,
* architectural RFCs,
* implementation guidance,
* operational procedures.

Specific governance rules are defined by future RFCs.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Documentation as Knowledge

Rejected because documentation may contain multiple unrelated concepts.

Knowledge Objects provide finer granularity and stronger traceability.

---

## Source Code as Knowledge

Rejected because source code explains implementation but not organizational reasoning.

---

## AI Memory

Rejected because AI memory is implementation-dependent and not under organizational ownership.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* preserved engineering knowledge,
* stronger onboarding,
* improved traceability,
* reusable organizational capability,
* reduced dependence on individuals.

Trade-offs include:

* additional governance,
* review effort,
* lifecycle management.

These trade-offs are accepted because they improve long-term organizational capability.
