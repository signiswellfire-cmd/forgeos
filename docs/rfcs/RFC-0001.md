# RFC-0001 — ForgeOS Genome

**RFC Number:** RFC-0001

**Title:** ForgeOS Genome

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC formally defines the ForgeOS Genome.

The Genome is the canonical specification that defines the permanent identity of a ForgeOS organization. It establishes the immutable organizational concepts that every ForgeOS implementation must preserve regardless of programming language, storage engine, user interface, AI provider, or deployment model.

The Genome is the root architectural specification from which all subsequent organizational models are derived.

---

# Motivation

Traditional software systems preserve source code.

ForgeOS preserves engineering organizations.

To preserve an organization, ForgeOS requires a stable representation of its identity that survives:

* implementation changes,
* infrastructure changes,
* personnel changes,
* AI model replacement,
* organizational growth.

Without such a representation, organizational capability becomes dependent on temporary implementation details rather than permanent engineering knowledge.

---

# Problem Statement

Organizations evolve continuously.

Projects begin and end.

People join and leave.

Technologies become obsolete.

AI models improve.

None of these events should redefine what the organization fundamentally is.

ForgeOS therefore requires a stable model that separates organizational identity from operational state.

---

# Goals

The Genome shall:

* define the permanent organizational identity;
* establish the core domain concepts of ForgeOS;
* provide a stable foundation for future RFCs;
* remain implementation independent;
* support long-term organizational evolution;
* preserve engineering knowledge across decades.

---

# Non-Goals

The Genome does **not** define:

* storage implementation;
* API design;
* desktop interfaces;
* database schema;
* AI integration;
* plugin architecture;
* deployment topology.

Those concerns are specified elsewhere.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the **Genome** as the highest-level organizational model.

Every ForgeOS organization is instantiated from a Genome.

Operational state changes over time.

The Genome changes only through deliberate architectural governance.

---

# Genome Model

The Genome consists of eleven first-class concepts.

## 1. Organization

Represents the permanent engineering organization.

Responsibilities include:

* organizational identity;
* governance;
* institutional memory;
* long-term objectives.

There is exactly one Organization per ForgeOS instance.

---

## 2. Professional

A Professional represents a permanent organizational responsibility.

Professionals are organizational roles rather than AI agents.

A Professional may be fulfilled by:

* a human,
* a local AI,
* a cloud AI,
* a hybrid execution model.

Responsibilities belong to the role—not the implementation.

---

## 3. Team

Teams organize Professionals around Missions.

Teams are temporary execution structures.

Knowledge remains organizational.

---

## 4. Mission

A Mission represents an organizational objective with measurable outcomes.

Every Mission:

* has ownership;
* progresses through a lifecycle;
* produces artifacts;
* generates organizational knowledge.

---

## 5. Process

Processes define repeatable organizational behavior.

Processes describe *how* work is performed.

Automation implements processes but does not replace them.

---

## 6. Knowledge

Knowledge is the primary organizational asset.

Examples include:

* architectural decisions;
* blueprints;
* engineering standards;
* lessons learned;
* validated experience.

Knowledge persists independently of implementation.

---

## 7. Blueprint

Blueprints describe reusable organizational solutions.

Blueprints may define:

* architectures;
* workflows;
* engineering patterns;
* organizational structures;
* implementation templates.

Blueprints enable organizational reuse.

---

## 8. Capability

Capabilities describe what an organization is able to accomplish.

Capabilities evolve continuously.

Capabilities improve through completed Missions and promoted Knowledge.

---

## 9. Decision

Decisions are permanent engineering knowledge objects.

Every significant decision should remain:

* traceable;
* explainable;
* versioned;
* reviewable.

---

## 10. Artifact

Artifacts are outputs produced during execution.

Examples include:

* documentation;
* source code;
* diagrams;
* RFCs;
* TDSs;
* TDRs;
* test suites.

Artifacts become organizational assets after validation and promotion.

---

## 11. Event

Events describe meaningful organizational change.

Examples include:

* MissionCreated
* KnowledgePromoted
* BlueprintPublished
* ProfessionalAssigned

Events support traceability.

Events are not considered the primary organizational knowledge store.

---

# Organizational Lifecycle

Every ForgeOS organization continuously performs the following cycle:

1. Establish organizational objectives.
2. Execute Missions.
3. Produce Artifacts.
4. Validate outcomes.
5. Promote Knowledge.
6. Improve Capabilities.
7. Strengthen the Organization.

The cycle repeats indefinitely.

The organization becomes progressively more capable over time.

---

# Design Principles

The Genome follows several architectural principles.

## Identity Before Activity

Identity should remain stable regardless of current work.

---

## Organization Before Individuals

Capabilities belong to the organization.

Individuals contribute to those capabilities.

---

## Knowledge Before Automation

Knowledge governs automation.

Automation never becomes the authoritative source.

---

## Stable Core

The Genome should evolve slowly.

Operational behavior evolves continuously.

---

## Explainability

Every Genome concept should remain understandable without reference to implementation technologies.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Repository-Centric Identity

Rejected because repositories preserve implementation rather than organizational identity.

---

## AI-Agent-Centric Model

Rejected because AI providers are replaceable implementation details.

Organizational responsibilities must remain technology independent.

---

## Event-Sourced Identity

Rejected for the initial architecture.

While events provide traceability, organizational identity should not depend upon reconstructing historical events.

Event sourcing may become an implementation detail in future versions without changing the Genome.

---

# Consequences

Positive consequences include:

* stable organizational identity;
* technology independence;
* reusable organizational capability;
* durable engineering knowledge;
* consistent architectural evolution.

Trade-offs include:

* additional domain modeling;
* stronger governance requirements;
* deliberate architectural evolution.

These trade-offs are accepted because they preserve long-term organizational integrity.
