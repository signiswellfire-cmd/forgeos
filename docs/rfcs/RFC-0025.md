# RFC-0025 — Organization Health

**RFC Number:** RFC-0025

**Title:** Organization Health

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Organization Health framework for ForgeOS.

Organization Health provides a measurable representation of the operational condition, engineering maturity, organizational capability, governance quality, and long-term sustainability of a ForgeOS Organization.

Unlike conventional dashboards that primarily report activity metrics, Organization Health evaluates whether the organization itself is becoming stronger over time.

---

# Motivation

Organizations frequently measure:

* velocity,
* sprint completion,
* issue count,
* deployment frequency,
* lines of code.

These metrics reveal operational activity but rarely indicate whether the engineering organization is improving.

ForgeOS measures organizational capability rather than implementation volume.

---

# Problem Statement

Without organizational health measurement:

* engineering debt accumulates unnoticed;
* governance gradually weakens;
* capability stagnates;
* knowledge quality declines;
* executives lack objective visibility into long-term organizational growth.

ForgeOS therefore requires a dedicated organizational health model.

---

# Goals

Organization Health shall:

* measure organizational capability;
* identify engineering risks early;
* evaluate governance effectiveness;
* monitor knowledge quality;
* guide organizational improvement;
* remain independent of implementation technology.

---

# Non-Goals

Organization Health does not replace:

* application monitoring;
* infrastructure monitoring;
* financial reporting;
* project management metrics;
* operational dashboards.

It evaluates the engineering organization itself.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces Organization Health as a permanent organizational capability.

Health indicators are derived from validated organizational knowledge, engineering activity, governance outcomes, and capability evolution rather than isolated operational metrics.

Health measurements should influence strategic planning rather than daily implementation decisions.

---

# Health Domains

Organization Health is evaluated across multiple domains.

## Knowledge Health

Evaluates:

* Knowledge Object quality;
* documentation completeness;
* Blueprint coverage;
* Executive Memory quality;
* Engineering Memory quality.

Objective:

Determine whether organizational knowledge is improving.

---

## Capability Health

Evaluates:

* capability maturity;
* capability growth;
* capability reuse;
* competency distribution.

Objective:

Measure organizational competence.

---

## Governance Health

Evaluates:

* decision traceability;
* standards compliance;
* architectural consistency;
* approval quality.

Objective:

Measure organizational discipline.

---

## Mission Health

Evaluates:

* Mission completion;
* validation success;
* Knowledge Promotion rate;
* organizational value delivered.

Objective:

Measure execution effectiveness.

---

## Workforce Health

Evaluates:

* Professional utilization;
* collaboration;
* role coverage;
* capability alignment;
* organizational resilience.

Objective:

Measure workforce effectiveness rather than individual productivity.

---

## Process Health

Evaluates:

* process adoption;
* governance compliance;
* improvement frequency;
* process stability.

Objective:

Measure operational maturity.

---

## Architecture Health

Evaluates:

* modularity;
* dependency integrity;
* standards compliance;
* architectural debt;
* RFC implementation coverage.

Objective:

Measure long-term architectural sustainability.

---

# Health Indicators

Representative indicators include:

* promoted Knowledge Objects;
* Blueprint reuse ratio;
* capability maturity growth;
* Mission success rate;
* architectural review completion;
* decision traceability coverage;
* standards compliance;
* process improvement frequency;
* organizational learning velocity.

Metrics should remain explainable and traceable.

---

# Health Assessment Cycle

Organization Health follows a continuous cycle.

```text
Observation
      ↓
Measurement
      ↓
Analysis
      ↓
Executive Review
      ↓
Improvement Planning
      ↓
Mission Creation
      ↓
Capability Growth
      ↓
Observation
```

Health assessment is continuous rather than periodic.

---

# Organizational Health Score

ForgeOS may derive an aggregate health score.

The score:

* summarizes organizational condition;
* supports executive planning;
* tracks long-term trends.

Individual domain scores remain more important than the aggregate score.

The score should never replace engineering judgment.

---

# Health Events

Health events occur when significant organizational changes are detected.

Examples include:

* sustained capability improvement;
* governance regression;
* documentation degradation;
* architectural risk increase;
* declining Blueprint reuse.

Health events become organizational knowledge.

---

# Relationship to Executive Meetings

Executive Meetings review Organization Health when making strategic decisions.

Health information informs:

* prioritization;
* capability investment;
* workforce planning;
* architectural improvement.

Health metrics support—not replace—executive judgment.

---

# Relationship to Missions

Organization Health influences Mission selection.

Health deficiencies may generate improvement Missions.

Mission outcomes subsequently influence Organization Health.

This creates a continuous organizational feedback loop.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Health indicators reference:

* Missions;
* Capabilities;
* Knowledge Objects;
* Standards;
* Decisions;
* Blueprints;
* Professionals.

Health therefore emerges from organizational relationships rather than isolated metrics.

---

# Governance

Health indicators must be:

* measurable;
* reproducible;
* explainable;
* versioned.

Metrics should evolve through architectural governance.

Historical health trends remain organizational assets.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Productivity Metrics Only

Rejected because productivity does not necessarily indicate organizational improvement.

---

## Financial KPIs

Rejected because financial performance alone cannot evaluate engineering capability.

---

## AI Performance Metrics

Rejected because ForgeOS evaluates Organizations rather than AI providers.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* measurable organizational maturity;
* proactive engineering improvement;
* stronger governance;
* improved executive visibility;
* continuous organizational evolution;
* explainable strategic planning.

Trade-offs include:

* additional measurement;
* governance overhead;
* metric maintenance.

These trade-offs are accepted because they significantly strengthen long-term organizational capability.
