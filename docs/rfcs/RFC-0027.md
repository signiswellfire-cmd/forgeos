# RFC-0027 — Skill Framework

**RFC Number:** RFC-0027

**Title:** Skill Framework

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Skill Framework for ForgeOS.

The Skill Framework establishes a standardized representation of executable abilities possessed by Professionals. While Responsibilities describe **what** a Professional is accountable for, and Capabilities describe **what the Organization can reliably accomplish**, Skills define **how a Professional performs specific work**.

Skills are measurable, evolvable, reusable organizational assets.

---

# Motivation

Organizations often blur the distinction between:

* responsibilities,
* competencies,
* capabilities,
* certifications,
* experience,
* technical skills.

This ambiguity complicates staffing, mission planning, organizational growth, and AI reasoning.

ForgeOS separates these concepts into independent architectural models.

---

# Problem Statement

Without a formal Skill Framework:

* workforce planning becomes subjective;
* Mission assignment becomes inconsistent;
* capability gaps remain hidden;
* training priorities become unclear;
* AI workforce reasoning becomes unreliable.

A consistent skill model is therefore required.

---

# Goals

The Skill Framework shall:

* define standardized Skills;
* separate Skills from Responsibilities;
* support capability development;
* improve Mission assignment;
* enable measurable workforce growth;
* remain technology independent.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* employee performance reviews;
* hiring policies;
* certifications;
* compensation;
* implementation technologies.

These concerns belong to other organizational systems.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS models every executable ability as a Skill.

Professionals possess Skills.

Capabilities emerge from the coordinated application of Skills across the Organization.

Skills may evolve without changing Professional identity.

---

# Skill Definition

Every Skill contains:

* Skill Identifier;
* Name;
* Description;
* Category;
* Prerequisites;
* Required Knowledge;
* Associated Competencies;
* Recommended Blueprints;
* Assessment Criteria;
* Maturity Level;
* Version History.

A Skill should represent a single coherent ability.

# Skill Levels

ForgeOS defines five maturity levels.

## Level 1 — Awareness

Basic understanding.

Requires supervision.

---

## Level 2 — Practitioner

Can perform routine work independently.

---

## Level 3 — Advanced

Can solve complex problems and mentor others.

---

## Level 4 — Expert

Recognized organizational authority.

Defines best practices.

---

## Level 5 — Distinguished

Shapes organizational direction and advances the discipline itself.

---

# Skill Acquisition

Skills develop through:

* Mission execution;
* Blueprint adoption;
* Knowledge Promotion;
* mentoring;
* experimentation;
* engineering practice.

Skill growth should be evidence-based.

---

# Skill Assessment

Skill assessment evaluates:

* demonstrated experience;
* engineering quality;
* repeatability;
* organizational impact;
* explainability.

Assessment emphasizes demonstrated competence over theoretical knowledge.

---

# Skill Composition

Complex work frequently requires combinations of Skills.

Example:

Software Architecture may require:

* Domain Modeling;
* API Design;
* Systems Thinking;
* Technical Communication;
* Risk Analysis.

The Organization reasons about Skill combinations when planning Missions.

---

# Relationship to Responsibilities

Responsibilities define obligations.

Skills enable execution.

Responsibilities remain stable.

Skills continuously evolve.

---

# Relationship to Competencies

Competencies group related Skills into broader domains of expertise.

Competencies provide organizational structure.

Skills provide execution detail.

---

# Relationship to Capabilities

Capabilities emerge when the Organization consistently applies Skills across multiple Professionals.

Individual Skills alone do not constitute organizational Capability.

---

# Relationship to Missions

Mission planning identifies required Skills.

Professional assignment considers Skill alignment in addition to Responsibilities and Authority.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Skills become graph nodes connected to:

* Professionals;
* Competencies;
* Capabilities;
* Missions;
* Blueprints;
* Knowledge Objects;
* Engineering Standards.

These relationships enable explainable workforce planning.

---

# Governance

Skill definitions require architectural governance.

Changes should preserve:

* semantic consistency;
* historical traceability;
* organizational comparability.

Skill proliferation without governance is prohibited.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Resume Skills

Rejected because personal resumes are inconsistent and not organizationally governed.

---

## Technology-Specific Skills

Rejected because technologies evolve more rapidly than organizational capabilities.

Skills should emphasize enduring engineering abilities.

---

## Competency-Only Model

Rejected because Competencies are too coarse to support detailed Mission planning.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* improved workforce planning;
* objective Skill assessment;
* stronger Mission assignment;
* measurable professional growth;
* explainable capability development.

Trade-offs include:

* governance effort;
* assessment maintenance;
* lifecycle management.

These trade-offs are accepted because they strengthen organizational execution.
