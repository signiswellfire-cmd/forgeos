# RFC-0019 — Blueprint Inheritance

**RFC Number:** RFC-0019

**Title:** Blueprint Inheritance

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Blueprint Inheritance model for ForgeOS.

Blueprint Inheritance enables Organizations to create specialized Blueprints from existing validated Blueprints while preserving architectural consistency, organizational traceability, and knowledge reuse.

Rather than duplicating engineering knowledge, ForgeOS allows organizations to extend proven organizational solutions through explicit inheritance.

Inheritance is an organizational evolution mechanism rather than a software inheritance mechanism.

---

# Motivation

Engineering organizations rarely create entirely new solutions.

Instead they adapt existing practices to fit:

* different industries,
* organization sizes,
* technology stacks,
* regulatory environments,
* deployment models,
* customer requirements.

Copying existing documentation leads to divergence and duplicated maintenance.

ForgeOS therefore models inheritance explicitly.

---

# Problem Statement

Without inheritance:

* Blueprint duplication increases,
* engineering standards drift,
* improvements fail to propagate,
* maintenance costs rise,
* organizational knowledge fragments.

Organizations require a controlled mechanism for specialization without duplication.

---

# Goals

Blueprint Inheritance shall:

* maximize knowledge reuse;
* minimize duplication;
* preserve architectural consistency;
* support specialization;
* maintain traceability;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

Blueprint Inheritance does not define:

* object-oriented inheritance,
* source code inheritance,
* template rendering,
* configuration management,
* programming language behavior.

This RFC concerns organizational knowledge.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS adopts explicit Blueprint Inheritance.

Every derived Blueprint shall reference exactly one parent Blueprint.

Inheritance relationships are permanent organizational knowledge and become part of the Knowledge Graph.

---

# Blueprint Hierarchy

Blueprints form a directed hierarchy.

Example:

```text
ForgeOS Architecture Blueprint
            │
            ├──────────────┐
            │              │
ERP Architecture      Game Architecture
            │              │
Manufacturing ERP     Multiplayer Game
```

Every Blueprint may have multiple descendants.

Each Blueprint has at most one immediate parent.

---

# Parent Blueprint

The parent Blueprint defines:

* architectural principles,
* organizational intent,
* reusable knowledge,
* inherited constraints,
* baseline governance.

Parents remain independently valid after descendants are created.

---

# Derived Blueprint

Derived Blueprints extend parent Blueprints by introducing:

* specialization,
* additional constraints,
* organization-specific guidance,
* technology recommendations,
* implementation adaptations.

Derived Blueprints should avoid redefining inherited concepts unless explicitly justified.

---

# Inheritance Rules

Blueprint inheritance follows these rules.

## Rule 1 — Preserve Intent

A derived Blueprint shall preserve the architectural intent of its parent.

---

## Rule 2 — Explicit Extension

Every deviation from the parent Blueprint shall be documented.

Hidden divergence is prohibited.

---

## Rule 3 — No Circular Relationships

Blueprint inheritance shall always form an acyclic graph.

Circular inheritance is invalid.

---

## Rule 4 — Independent Validity

Every Blueprint shall remain independently understandable.

Understanding a derived Blueprint should not require reading every ancestor in detail.

---

## Rule 5 — Traceability

Inheritance relationships shall remain permanently traceable.

The Organization should always know:

* where a Blueprint originated,
* why it was specialized,
* which organizational needs it addresses.

---

# Inheritance Metadata

Each inherited Blueprint records:

* parent Blueprint,
* inheritance date,
* inheritance rationale,
* inherited capabilities,
* overridden sections,
* additional constraints,
* stewardship,
* version history.

---

# Organizational Benefits

Blueprint Inheritance enables:

* consistent engineering practices,
* faster organizational growth,
* reusable organizational knowledge,
* reduced maintenance effort,
* explainable specialization.

Inheritance strengthens organizational evolution rather than increasing documentation.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Promotion

A promoted Blueprint may become the parent of future Blueprints.

Knowledge Promotion therefore creates reusable organizational foundations.

Inheritance extends those foundations.

---

# Relationship to Capability Development

Derived Blueprints may create:

* new organizational Capabilities,
* specialized Competencies,
* refined engineering Processes.

Capability evolution should remain traceable through Blueprint lineage.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Blueprint inheritance introduces relationships including:

* inherits_from,
* specializes,
* extends,
* supersedes,
* derives_from.

These relationships improve organizational reasoning and reuse.

---

# Governance

Blueprint inheritance requires architectural review.

Inheritance should occur only when:

* specialization is justified,
* duplication is avoided,
* architectural consistency is preserved.

Parent Blueprints remain protected organizational assets.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Copy-and-Modify Documentation

Rejected because duplicated documentation rapidly diverges.

---

## Independent Blueprints

Rejected because relationships between organizational solutions become invisible.

---

## Multiple Parent Inheritance

Rejected because it increases complexity and creates conflicting organizational guidance.

Single-parent inheritance provides sufficient flexibility while preserving clarity.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* reduced duplication;
* improved maintainability;
* stronger organizational consistency;
* reusable engineering knowledge;
* traceable organizational evolution.

Trade-offs include:

* governance requirements;
* inheritance management;
* architectural review effort.

These trade-offs are accepted because they strengthen long-term organizational capability.
