# RFC-0004 — Organization Model

**RFC Number:** RFC-0004

**Title:** Organization Model

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the canonical Organization Model of ForgeOS.

The Organization Model specifies how a digital engineering organization is represented, governed, structured, and evolved. It formalizes the relationship between organizations, professionals, teams, missions, capabilities, processes, and knowledge.

This RFC establishes the organizational architecture upon which every ForgeOS implementation is built.

---

# Motivation

ForgeOS is not a task manager, AI agent framework, or software development environment.

It is a **Digital Organization Operating System**.

Consequently, the organization—not the application—is the primary architectural unit.

Traditional software platforms organize work around users, tickets, or projects.

ForgeOS organizes work around a continuously evolving engineering organization.

---

# Problem Statement

Conventional development platforms typically treat organizational structure as an external concern.

Engineering knowledge, responsibilities, and decision authority become scattered across:

* project management systems,
* documentation,
* communication platforms,
* individual contributors.

This fragmentation weakens governance, onboarding, accountability, and organizational learning.

ForgeOS requires a first-class organizational model to preserve institutional capability.

---

# Goals

The Organization Model shall:

* define organizational structure;
* formalize engineering responsibilities;
* preserve institutional knowledge;
* support organizational evolution;
* remain implementation independent;
* provide the foundation for mission execution.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* authentication,
* authorization,
* UI hierarchy,
* persistence strategy,
* workflow implementation,
* AI execution.

These concerns belong to future specifications.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS models every engineering organization as a hierarchy of permanent organizational entities connected through explicit responsibilities rather than implementation technologies.

The organization itself becomes the primary unit of architecture.

---

# Organizational Hierarchy

The initial hierarchy is:

```text
Organization
├── Professionals
├── Teams
├── Missions
├── Processes
├── Capabilities
├── Knowledge
├── Blueprints
├── Decisions
└── Artifacts
```

This hierarchy represents ownership rather than execution order.

---

# Organization

The Organization is the root entity.

Responsibilities include:

* governance,
* organizational identity,
* engineering standards,
* institutional memory,
* strategic objectives.

Each ForgeOS instance contains exactly one Organization.

---

# Professionals

Professionals represent permanent organizational responsibilities.

A Professional is defined by:

* role,
* competencies,
* authority,
* responsibilities,
* capabilities,
* organizational memory.

Professionals are organizational abstractions.

Execution may be performed by:

* humans,
* local AI,
* cloud AI,
* hybrid systems.

The role remains constant regardless of implementation.

---

# Teams

Teams are temporary execution structures assembled to accomplish Missions.

Teams:

* have defined objectives,
* contain Professionals,
* dissolve after mission completion.

Knowledge remains with the Organization rather than the Team.

---

# Missions

A Mission is the smallest independently managed organizational objective.

Every Mission shall include:

* objective,
* owner,
* lifecycle,
* priority,
* dependencies,
* deliverables,
* outcomes,
* promoted knowledge.

Mission completion should strengthen organizational capability.

---

# Capabilities

Capabilities describe what the organization can reliably accomplish.

Capabilities evolve through:

* successful missions,
* promoted knowledge,
* validated experience,
* improved standards.

Capabilities belong to the Organization.

---

# Processes

Processes define repeatable organizational behavior.

Processes describe:

* activities,
* responsibilities,
* decision points,
* validation,
* outputs.

Automation implements Processes but does not define them.

---

# Knowledge

Knowledge represents permanent organizational assets.

Knowledge survives:

* personnel changes,
* implementation changes,
* AI replacement,
* infrastructure migration.

Knowledge remains organizational property.

---

# Blueprints

Blueprints encapsulate reusable organizational solutions.

Blueprints may represent:

* architectures,
* workflows,
* engineering patterns,
* organizational structures,
* implementation templates.

Blueprints accelerate future Missions.

---

# Decisions

Every significant engineering decision becomes a permanent organizational asset.

Decisions should remain:

* explainable,
* versioned,
* traceable,
* reviewable.

Decision governance is defined by future RFCs.

---

# Artifacts

Artifacts represent outputs produced during Mission execution.

Examples include:

* source code,
* RFCs,
* TDSs,
* documentation,
* diagrams,
* tests,
* deployment assets.

Artifacts become permanent organizational knowledge through promotion.

---

# Organizational Lifecycle

Organizations evolve through a continuous improvement loop.

```text
Vision
    ↓
Mission Planning
    ↓
Mission Execution
    ↓
Artifact Creation
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Knowledge Promotion
    ↓
Capability Improvement
    ↓
Organizational Evolution
```

The objective is organizational growth rather than merely project completion.

---

# Authority Model

Authority follows organizational responsibilities rather than implementation.

Typical authority levels include:

* Founder
* Executive
* Professional
* Team
* Mission

Authority may be delegated but remains explicitly defined.

Detailed authority rules are specified by future RFCs.

---

# Organizational Evolution

Organizations evolve by:

* improving capabilities,
* refining processes,
* promoting knowledge,
* introducing new professionals,
* publishing blueprints.

Evolution should strengthen organizational capability without redefining organizational identity.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## User-Centric Model

Rejected because ForgeOS manages organizations rather than individual users.

---

## AI-Agent Model

Rejected because AI implementations are replaceable while organizational responsibilities are permanent.

---

## Project-Centric Model

Rejected because projects are temporary.

Organizations are long-lived.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* explicit organizational governance;
* reusable engineering capability;
* durable institutional knowledge;
* technology-independent architecture;
* scalable engineering organizations.

Trade-offs include:

* richer domain model;
* stronger governance;
* increased architectural discipline.

These trade-offs are accepted because they improve long-term organizational effectiveness.
