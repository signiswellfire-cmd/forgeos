# RFC-0006 — Executive Meeting Protocol

**RFC Number:** RFC-0006

**Title:** Executive Meeting Protocol

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Executive Meeting Protocol, the primary strategic coordination mechanism within a ForgeOS organization.

Unlike traditional AI orchestration systems where agents communicate directly with one another, ForgeOS models strategic coordination as structured executive meetings. Every significant organizational decision is expected to emerge from explicit deliberation rather than implicit interaction.

The Executive Meeting Protocol creates explainable, auditable, and governable organizational reasoning.

---

# Motivation

Engineering organizations continuously make decisions regarding:

* priorities,
* architecture,
* staffing,
* risk,
* execution,
* quality,
* investment.

Traditional AI systems often hide these decisions inside opaque reasoning processes.

ForgeOS instead exposes strategic reasoning as an organizational process that mirrors how mature engineering organizations operate.

---

# Problem Statement

Without structured executive coordination:

* architectural decisions become inconsistent,
* competing priorities remain unresolved,
* organizational reasoning cannot be reviewed,
* AI behavior becomes difficult to explain,
* governance becomes dependent upon implementation details.

ForgeOS requires an explicit protocol for collaborative decision making.

---

# Goals

The Executive Meeting Protocol shall:

* coordinate organizational leadership;
* preserve strategic reasoning;
* support explainable decisions;
* record alternatives;
* enable Founder oversight;
* remain independent of AI providers.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* conversational interfaces,
* user interface design,
* AI prompting,
* LLM orchestration,
* scheduling implementation,
* notification systems.

These concerns belong to implementation specifications.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS adopts the Executive Meeting as the canonical mechanism for strategic organizational reasoning.

Significant organizational decisions should be discussed through structured meetings rather than isolated agent interactions.

The meeting becomes a permanent organizational artifact.

---

# Meeting Participants

Participants are organizational Professionals.

Examples include:

* Founder
* Chief Executive Officer
* Chief Software Architect
* Product Manager
* Engineering Manager
* Quality Engineer
* Security Officer
* Technical Writer

Participation depends upon the decision under consideration.

Professionals contribute expertise rather than authority beyond their defined responsibilities.

---

# Meeting Lifecycle

Every Executive Meeting progresses through the following stages.

## 1. Agenda Creation

A meeting begins with one or more organizational objectives.

Examples:

* approve architecture,
* resolve engineering risk,
* prioritize missions,
* review organizational health.

---

## 2. Context Collection

Relevant organizational knowledge is assembled.

Sources include:

* RFCs,
* TDSs,
* TDRs,
* engineering standards,
* mission status,
* organizational capabilities,
* historical decisions.

Participants begin discussion with a shared context.

---

## 3. Individual Analysis

Each Professional independently evaluates the agenda using responsibilities, competencies, and available knowledge.

Individual reasoning should remain attributable to the Professional rather than merged into anonymous organizational output.

---

## 4. Deliberation

Professionals exchange viewpoints.

Deliberation should identify:

* agreements,
* disagreements,
* assumptions,
* trade-offs,
* risks,
* missing information.

Constructive disagreement is encouraged.

---

## 5. Recommendation

The meeting produces one or more recommendations.

Each recommendation includes:

* proposed decision,
* supporting rationale,
* expected consequences,
* alternatives considered,
* confidence level.

Recommendations remain explainable.

---

## 6. Approval

Authority is determined by organizational governance.

Some decisions may require:

* Founder approval,
* Executive approval,
* delegated Professional authority.

Approval rules are defined independently from the meeting protocol.

---

## 7. Decision Recording

Approved outcomes become permanent organizational Decisions.

Decision records reference:

* participants,
* agenda,
* rationale,
* supporting knowledge,
* implementation impact.

---

## 8. Mission Creation

Approved decisions may generate one or more Missions.

Mission creation begins the Forge Pipeline.

---

# Meeting Principles

Executive Meetings follow several principles.

## Structured Reasoning

Reasoning should remain explicit.

Hidden decision making is discouraged.

---

## Knowledge First

Participants reason from organizational knowledge before proposing new ideas.

Existing knowledge should always be considered before introducing new solutions.

---

## Professional Responsibility

Professionals contribute according to defined organizational responsibilities.

Authority does not imply expertise.

Expertise does not imply authority.

Both are modeled independently.

---

## Challenge Before Agreement

Participants are expected to challenge assumptions respectfully.

Consensus should emerge from evidence rather than convenience.

Constructive disagreement strengthens organizational capability.

---

## Explainability

Every recommendation should remain understandable months or years later.

Meeting records should preserve:

* context,
* reasoning,
* alternatives,
* consequences.

---

# Outputs

An Executive Meeting may produce:

* Decisions,
* Missions,
* Risks,
* Architectural reviews,
* Knowledge promotion requests,
* Capability improvement initiatives,
* Process improvements.

Outputs become organizational assets after approval.

---

# Governance

Executive Meetings do not replace organizational authority.

They support informed decision making.

Final authority remains governed by the Organization Model and future authority RFCs.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Direct AI Agent Communication

Rejected because reasoning becomes difficult to audit and govern.

---

## Founder-Centric Decision Making

Rejected because organizations should distribute expertise while preserving governance.

---

## Informal Collaboration

Rejected because undocumented reasoning weakens institutional memory.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* explainable organizational reasoning,
* improved architectural governance,
* reusable decision history,
* reduced knowledge loss,
* stronger institutional learning.

Trade-offs include:

* additional governance,
* structured deliberation,
* decision documentation overhead.

These trade-offs are accepted because they improve long-term organizational capability.
