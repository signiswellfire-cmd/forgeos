# RFC-0034 — Organization DNA

**RFC Number:** RFC-0034

**Title:** Organization DNA

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Organization DNA model, the immutable and slowly evolving identity layer of a ForgeOS Organization.

Organization DNA represents the fundamental characteristics that distinguish one Organization from another. It encodes enduring organizational identity including culture, operating philosophy, governance style, engineering principles, risk tolerance, decision preferences, collaboration patterns, and long-term behavioral characteristics.

Unlike operational state, Organization DNA changes infrequently and provides continuity across years of organizational evolution.

---

# Motivation

Organizations often document:

* vision;
* mission;
* values;
* engineering standards;
* processes.

However, these documents do not fully explain *how* an organization consistently behaves.

Two organizations with identical technology stacks and organizational charts may produce dramatically different outcomes because their organizational DNA differs.

ForgeOS models these enduring characteristics explicitly.

---

# Problem Statement

Without an explicit organizational identity model:

* culture becomes implicit;
* strategic decisions become inconsistent;
* workforce evolution lacks direction;
* organizational behavior becomes difficult to reproduce;
* AI reasoning cannot consistently represent organizational preferences.

ForgeOS requires a persistent identity model independent of implementation technologies.

---

# Goals

The Organization DNA shall:

* define enduring organizational identity;
* guide strategic decision making;
* preserve organizational consistency;
* influence workforce evolution;
* support explainable organizational reasoning;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

Organization DNA does not define:

* temporary objectives;
* project plans;
* Mission execution;
* implementation technologies;
* infrastructure;
* software architecture.

These concerns belong to operational layers.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces Organization DNA as the highest-level organizational identity artifact.

Every Organization shall possess exactly one active Organization DNA profile.

The DNA profile governs long-term organizational behavior while allowing operational adaptation.

---

# DNA Principles

## Identity Before Optimization

Operational improvements shall preserve organizational identity.

Optimization should never unintentionally redefine the Organization.

---

## Stability Before Change

Organization DNA evolves deliberately.

Frequent changes reduce organizational coherence.

---

## Explainability

Every DNA characteristic shall have documented rationale and organizational evidence.

---

## Governance

Changes to Organization DNA require executive approval.

DNA evolution is considered a strategic organizational event.

---

## Inheritance

Organizations may derive DNA from parent organizations while explicitly documenting deviations.

Inheritance preserves organizational lineage.

---

# DNA Components

Organization DNA consists of the following components.

## Purpose

The enduring reason the Organization exists.

---

## Core Values

Permanent organizational values.

Examples:

* craftsmanship;
* transparency;
* curiosity;
* customer success;
* continuous learning.

---

## Operating Principles

Rules governing organizational behavior.

Examples:

* Knowledge Before AI
* Governance Before Automation
* Organization Before Software

---

## Decision Philosophy

Defines preferred decision-making approaches.

Examples:

* evidence-driven;
* consensus-oriented;
* founder-led;
* experimentation-first.

---

## Engineering Philosophy

Defines long-term engineering beliefs.

Examples:

* modularity;
* local-first;
* documentation-first;
* architecture-centric development.

---

## Collaboration Style

Defines how Professionals interact.

Representative models include:

* hierarchical;
* decentralized;
* mission-oriented;
* capability-driven.

---

## Risk Profile

Defines acceptable organizational risk.

Dimensions include:

* innovation tolerance;
* operational risk;
* technical debt tolerance;
* experimentation level.

---

## Learning Philosophy

Defines how organizational learning occurs.

Examples:

* Experience Capture;
* Blueprint reuse;
* mentoring;
* continuous experimentation.

---

# DNA Lifecycle

Organization DNA progresses through:

1. Definition
2. Executive Review
3. Approval
4. Active
5. Evolution
6. Supersession
7. Archive

Identity changes should remain rare and fully traceable.

---

# DNA Evolution

Organization DNA evolves through:

* executive strategic decisions;
* validated organizational learning;
* major organizational transformation;
* mergers or structural integration.

Operational experience alone should not redefine organizational identity.

---

# DNA Inheritance

Child Organizations may inherit DNA from parent Organizations.

Inherited characteristics remain explicit.

Organizations may override selected characteristics while preserving lineage.

Inheritance relationships become permanent organizational knowledge.

---

# Relationship to Vision and Mission

Vision describes the desired future.

Mission describes the organizational purpose.

Organization DNA describes *how* the Organization consistently behaves while pursuing both.

These concepts are complementary.

---

# Relationship to the Organizational Intelligence Engine

The Organizational Intelligence Engine uses DNA as a stable reasoning constraint.

Recommendations should align with organizational identity.

Recommendations conflicting with DNA should be explicitly identified.

---

# Relationship to Strategic Planning

Strategic Planning generates objectives consistent with Organization DNA.

Strategic optimization should strengthen rather than dilute identity.

---

# Relationship to the Digital Twin

The Organization Digital Twin contains the active DNA profile as a foundational organizational attribute.

The Digital Twin represents both:

* current operational state;
* enduring organizational identity.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Organization DNA becomes a first-class Knowledge Graph node connected to:

* Vision;
* Mission;
* Executive Decisions;
* Engineering Principles;
* Blueprints;
* Capabilities;
* Organization Health.

These relationships preserve explainable organizational evolution.

---

# Governance

Organization DNA requires:

* executive sponsorship;
* documented rationale;
* historical traceability;
* controlled evolution;
* organizational consensus where appropriate.

Unauthorized DNA modification is prohibited.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Culture Documents

Rejected because culture documentation lacks formal structure and governance.

---

## Corporate Values Only

Rejected because values alone do not adequately describe organizational behavior.

---

## AI Personality Profiles

Rejected because AI personalities describe implementation behavior rather than organizational identity.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* preserved organizational identity;
* consistent executive decisions;
* explainable strategic reasoning;
* stronger organizational continuity;
* improved AI alignment;
* durable organizational evolution.

Trade-offs include:

* executive governance effort;
* slower identity evolution;
* additional organizational modeling.

These trade-offs are accepted because they protect long-term organizational coherence.
