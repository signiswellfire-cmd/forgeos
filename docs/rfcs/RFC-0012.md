# RFC-0012 — Experience Capture

**RFC Number:** RFC-0012

**Title:** Experience Capture

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Experience Capture framework of ForgeOS.

Experience Capture is the structured process by which temporary execution experience is transformed into permanent organizational learning. While Knowledge Promotion governs how validated knowledge becomes a permanent organizational asset, Experience Capture governs how observations, lessons, and engineering insights are collected before they are evaluated.

Experience is the raw material from which organizational wisdom is created.

---

# Motivation

Every engineering activity produces experience.

Examples include:

* successful architecture decisions,
* failed implementations,
* production incidents,
* customer feedback,
* engineering retrospectives,
* experimentation,
* operational improvements.

Most organizations lose this experience because it remains embedded in conversations, issue trackers, or individual memory.

ForgeOS intentionally captures experience before it disappears.

---

# Problem Statement

Without systematic experience capture:

* mistakes are repeated,
* successful practices remain undocumented,
* organizational learning becomes accidental,
* engineering maturity grows slowly,
* valuable context disappears after projects end.

An organization cannot continuously improve if its experience is transient.

---

# Goals

Experience Capture shall:

* preserve valuable execution experience;
* support organizational learning;
* provide inputs for Knowledge Promotion;
* improve future decision making;
* remain lightweight during execution;
* preserve context without prematurely creating permanent knowledge.

---

# Non-Goals

Experience Capture does not:

* automatically create Knowledge Objects;
* replace engineering documentation;
* replace post-implementation reviews;
* determine architectural correctness;
* bypass Knowledge Promotion governance.

Captured experience is not automatically organizational truth.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces **Experience Records** as temporary but structured organizational artifacts.

Experience Records are collected continuously throughout Mission execution.

Only validated Experience Records may later become permanent organizational knowledge.

---

# Experience Record

Each Experience Record contains:

* unique identifier,
* originating Mission,
* authoring Professional,
* timestamp,
* context,
* observation,
* outcome,
* supporting evidence,
* confidence level,
* recommended follow-up,
* lifecycle state.

Experience Records should remain concise while preserving sufficient context for future evaluation.

---

# Experience Categories

## Success

Practices that produced desirable outcomes.

---

## Failure

Practices that produced undesirable outcomes.

Failures are considered valuable organizational assets when properly documented.

---

## Observation

Neutral findings that may influence future engineering decisions.

---

## Experiment

Results from intentional exploration.

Experiments may validate or invalidate engineering assumptions.

---

## Risk

Newly identified organizational or technical risks.

---

## Opportunity

Potential improvements discovered during execution.

---

# Lifecycle

Experience Records progress through:

1. Captured
2. Reviewed
3. Classified
4. Evaluated
5. Promoted or Archived

Most Experience Records will eventually be archived.

Only a small percentage should become permanent Knowledge Objects.

---

# Capture Timing

Experience should be captured as close as possible to the originating event.

Capture opportunities include:

* Executive Meetings,
* Mission execution,
* code review,
* production incidents,
* architecture discussions,
* retrospectives,
* customer interactions.

Delayed capture reduces accuracy.

---

# Relationship to Missions

Every Mission should produce Experience Records.

Mission completion is expected to generate organizational learning regardless of implementation success.

A failed Mission may generate more valuable experience than a successful one.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Promotion

Experience Capture precedes Knowledge Promotion.

Typical flow:

```text
Mission
    ↓
Experience Record
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Knowledge Promotion
```

Experience should never bypass organizational validation.

---

# Relationship to Executive Memory

Strategically significant Experience Records may contribute to Executive Memory after validation.

Executive Memory references the promoted knowledge rather than the raw Experience Record.

---

# Governance

Experience Capture should encourage openness rather than blame.

The objective is organizational improvement.

Experience quality should be evaluated independently of Mission outcome.

Engineers should be rewarded for documenting valuable failures.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Retrospectives Only

Rejected because valuable observations occur continuously rather than exclusively after Mission completion.

---

## Chat History

Rejected because conversations are difficult to search, govern, validate, and preserve.

---

## Automatic AI Summarization

Rejected because organizational learning requires deliberate review rather than automatic generation.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* stronger organizational learning;
* richer Knowledge Promotion candidates;
* improved engineering quality;
* preserved institutional experience;
* reduced repeated mistakes.

Trade-offs include:

* additional documentation effort;
* review overhead;
* lifecycle management.

These trade-offs are accepted because they create durable organizational capability.
