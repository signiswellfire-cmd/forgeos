# RFC-0017 — Knowledge Ownership

**RFC Number:** RFC-0017

**Title:** Knowledge Ownership

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the ownership model for all permanent knowledge within ForgeOS.

Knowledge Ownership specifies who owns organizational knowledge, who may modify it, who governs its lifecycle, and how ownership changes over time.

The objective is to ensure that organizational knowledge survives implementation changes, personnel changes, AI provider changes, and organizational growth without ambiguity.

---

# Motivation

Engineering organizations frequently lose valuable knowledge because it becomes associated with individuals rather than the organization.

Examples include:

* undocumented architectural decisions,
* employee-specific expertise,
* AI session memory,
* personal notes,
* isolated documentation.

ForgeOS treats knowledge as an organizational asset rather than personal property.

---

# Problem Statement

Without a formal ownership model:

* knowledge becomes fragmented,
* contributors become bottlenecks,
* institutional memory deteriorates,
* architectural consistency weakens,
* organizational capability depends on individuals.

Permanent knowledge must therefore have explicit organizational ownership.

---

# Goals

Knowledge Ownership shall:

* establish organizational ownership;
* separate ownership from authorship;
* define stewardship responsibilities;
* support long-term governance;
* preserve historical attribution;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* file permissions,
* authentication,
* access control,
* repository hosting,
* security policies.

Those concerns belong to implementation.

---

# Decision

All permanent knowledge within ForgeOS is owned by the Organization.

Individuals, Professionals, AI systems, or external contributors may create knowledge, but ownership is transferred to the Organization once the knowledge is promoted.

Ownership never belongs to a specific AI provider or implementation technology.

---

# Ownership Principles

## Organizational Ownership

Every approved Knowledge Object belongs to the Organization.

Ownership survives:

* personnel changes,
* implementation rewrites,
* infrastructure migration,
* AI provider replacement.

---

## Authorship

Authorship records who originally created knowledge.

Authorship is historical metadata.

It does not determine ownership.

---

## Stewardship

Each Knowledge Object has one or more stewards.

Stewards are responsible for:

* maintaining accuracy,
* reviewing updates,
* coordinating evolution,
* preserving consistency.

Stewardship may change over time.

Ownership does not.

---

## Governance

Knowledge governance determines:

* promotion,
* revision,
* deprecation,
* archival,
* supersession.

Governance follows organizational authority rather than individual preference.

---

# Ownership Lifecycle

Knowledge ownership progresses through:

1. Creation
2. Review
3. Validation
4. Promotion
5. Organizational Ownership
6. Maintenance
7. Deprecation
8. Archival

Ownership becomes organizational at Promotion.

---

# Ownership Hierarchy

ForgeOS recognizes several ownership concepts.

## Creator

Original author.

Historical attribution only.

---

## Reviewer

Validates technical correctness.

---

## Steward

Maintains ongoing quality.

---

## Organization

Permanent owner.

Every promoted Knowledge Object has exactly one organizational owner.

---

# Transfer of Stewardship

Stewardship may change because of:

* organizational restructuring,
* capability evolution,
* professional reassignment,
* workload balancing.

Historical stewardship should remain traceable.

Ownership remains unchanged.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Promotion

Knowledge Promotion transfers validated knowledge into permanent organizational ownership.

Promotion therefore represents both:

* quality validation,
* ownership transition.

Promotion without ownership transfer is prohibited.

---

# Relationship to Executive Memory

Executive Memory summarizes organizational knowledge.

Executive Memory references owned Knowledge Objects rather than owning them independently.

This prevents duplication and conflicting authority.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Ownership relationships become graph edges.

Representative relationships include:

* owned_by,
* stewarded_by,
* created_by,
* reviewed_by,
* superseded_by.

These relationships support explainable organizational governance.

---

# Governance

Organizational ownership requires:

* documented lifecycle,
* version history,
* review traceability,
* stewardship assignment,
* organizational approval.

Knowledge should never exist without identifiable organizational ownership.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Personal Ownership

Rejected because organizations outlive individual contributors.

---

## Repository Ownership

Rejected because repositories store artifacts rather than govern organizational knowledge.

---

## AI Ownership

Rejected because AI providers are replaceable implementation technologies.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* preserved institutional memory;
* reduced knowledge silos;
* stronger governance;
* improved organizational resilience;
* explainable ownership.

Trade-offs include:

* stewardship management,
* governance overhead,
* lifecycle administration.

These trade-offs are accepted because they strengthen long-term organizational capability.
