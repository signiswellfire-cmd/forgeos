# RFC-0026 — Professional Framework

**RFC Number:** RFC-0026

**Title:** Professional Framework

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Professional Framework, the canonical model governing every Professional within a ForgeOS Organization.

A Professional is the smallest permanent execution entity within ForgeOS. Professionals represent organizational responsibilities rather than implementation technologies. Whether a Professional is performed by a human, a local AI model, a cloud AI provider, or a hybrid workforce is an implementation concern.

The Professional Framework standardizes how Professionals are defined, governed, evolved, measured, and integrated into the Organization.

---

# Motivation

Engineering organizations are fundamentally composed of Professionals rather than software components.

Traditional AI systems model execution around agents.

Traditional enterprise software models execution around users.

ForgeOS models execution around Professionals with clearly defined organizational responsibilities.

This distinction preserves organizational continuity while allowing execution technology to evolve.

---

# Problem Statement

Without a formal Professional model:

* responsibilities become ambiguous;
* capability planning becomes inconsistent;
* governance weakens;
* organizational knowledge fragments;
* workforce evolution becomes difficult;
* AI implementations leak into organizational architecture.

ForgeOS requires Professionals to be stable organizational entities independent of implementation.

---

# Goals

The Professional Framework shall:

* define permanent organizational roles;
* separate responsibilities from implementation;
* support capability-driven execution;
* enable organizational governance;
* preserve explainability;
* remain technology independent.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* employment contracts;
* authentication;
* payroll;
* identity management;
* AI prompting;
* implementation technologies.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the Professional as the canonical execution entity of the Organization.

Every execution activity is performed by one or more Professionals.

Every Professional is defined by organizational responsibility rather than execution technology.

---

# Professional Definition

Every Professional contains:

* Professional Identifier;
* Title;
* Organizational Purpose;
* Responsibilities;
* Authority Level;
* Competencies;
* Required Skills;
* Supported Capabilities;
* Mission Eligibility;
* Performance Indicators;
* Organizational Relationships;
* Lifecycle State.

These characteristics define the Professional regardless of implementation.

---

# Professional Responsibilities

Responsibilities represent permanent organizational expectations.

Examples include:

* designing architecture;
* reviewing code;
* planning Missions;
* validating quality;
* documenting knowledge;
* managing releases.

Responsibilities should remain stable even when execution technology changes.

---

# Professional Authority

Authority is governed by the Decision Authority Matrix.

Authority determines:

* decisions that may be approved;
* recommendations that may be issued;
* governance responsibilities;
* review obligations.

Authority is independent from intelligence.

---

# Professional Competencies

Competencies describe areas of organizational expertise.

Representative competencies include:

* Software Architecture;
* Backend Engineering;
* Product Strategy;
* Security Engineering;
* Knowledge Engineering;
* Organizational Governance.

Competencies evolve through organizational learning.

---

# Professional Lifecycle

Every Professional progresses through:

1. Definition
2. Approval
3. Activation
4. Assignment
5. Mission Participation
6. Capability Development
7. Organizational Evolution
8. Retirement

Lifecycle transitions remain governed organizational events.

---

# Professional Assignment

Professionals are assigned to Missions according to:

* required Capabilities;
* competency alignment;
* authority requirements;
* organizational priorities;
* workload constraints.

Assignments optimize organizational outcomes rather than resource utilization alone.

---

# Professional Collaboration

Professionals collaborate through:

* Executive Meetings;
* Mission execution;
* organizational Processes;
* shared Knowledge;
* Blueprint reuse.

Collaboration occurs through organizational governance rather than direct implementation coupling.

---

# Professional Performance

Performance is evaluated through organizational contribution.

Representative indicators include:

* Mission success;
* Knowledge contribution;
* Blueprint creation;
* Capability growth;
* engineering quality;
* governance participation;
* organizational impact.

Performance evaluation should encourage long-term organizational improvement.

---

# Relationship to Capabilities

Professionals contribute to organizational Capabilities.

Capabilities belong to the Organization.

Professional growth strengthens organizational capability rather than personal ownership.

---

# Relationship to Skills

Skills enable Professionals to execute responsibilities.

Skills are implementation-oriented.

Responsibilities remain organizational.

Future RFCs define the Skill Framework.

---

# Relationship to Teams

Teams are temporary organizational structures composed of Professionals.

Professionals remain permanent organizational entities.

Team dissolution never affects Professional identity.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Professionals become graph nodes connected to:

* Missions;
* Capabilities;
* Decisions;
* Knowledge Objects;
* Blueprints;
* Processes;
* Executive Meetings.

These relationships preserve organizational reasoning and explainability.

---

# Governance

Professional definitions require architectural governance.

Changes to responsibilities, authority, or organizational purpose require documented approval.

Implementation changes do not alter Professional identity.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## AI Agent Model

Rejected because AI agents represent implementation technologies rather than organizational responsibilities.

---

## User-Centric Model

Rejected because users interact with the Organization but do not define its execution model.

---

## Service-Oriented Workforce

Rejected because software services cannot adequately represent organizational governance.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* stable organizational structure;
* provider independence;
* improved explainability;
* stronger governance;
* scalable workforce evolution;
* durable organizational identity.

Trade-offs include:

* richer organizational modeling;
* governance overhead;
* competency management.

These trade-offs are accepted because they strengthen long-term organizational capability.
