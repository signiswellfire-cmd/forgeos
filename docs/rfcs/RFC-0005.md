# RFC-0005 — Forge Pipeline

**RFC Number:** RFC-0005

**Title:** Forge Pipeline

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Forge Pipeline, the canonical engineering lifecycle of ForgeOS.

Unlike traditional software development pipelines that terminate with deployment, the Forge Pipeline is designed to continuously strengthen the engineering organization itself.

Every execution cycle should leave the organization more capable than before through the creation, validation, and promotion of permanent organizational knowledge.

The pipeline is therefore the operational mechanism through which ForgeOS fulfills its philosophy:

> **AI is temporary. Knowledge is permanent.**

---

# Motivation

Conventional engineering pipelines optimize for delivering software.

ForgeOS optimizes for building engineering organizations.

Deployment is not the end of engineering work.

The true output of engineering is:

* improved organizational capability,
* reusable knowledge,
* validated decisions,
* stronger engineering standards.

Software is one artifact of that process.

---

# Problem Statement

Most development processes lose valuable engineering knowledge because they conclude after implementation.

Common outcomes include:

* undocumented design decisions,
* repeated architectural mistakes,
* isolated engineering experience,
* inconsistent standards,
* limited organizational learning.

The organization improves far more slowly than the software it produces.

ForgeOS requires a pipeline that explicitly converts execution into permanent organizational capability.

---

# Goals

The Forge Pipeline shall:

* standardize organizational execution;
* preserve engineering knowledge;
* produce reusable organizational assets;
* continuously improve capabilities;
* support explainable engineering;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* CI/CD implementation;
* deployment infrastructure;
* source control workflows;
* task management software;
* programming language tooling.

Those concerns are implementation details.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS adopts the **Forge Pipeline** as the canonical engineering lifecycle.

Every Mission progresses through a series of stages that transform ideas into permanent organizational knowledge.

Completion is measured not only by delivered software but also by strengthened organizational capability.

---

# Pipeline Overview

The canonical pipeline is:

```text
Idea
 ↓
Discovery
 ↓
Architecture
 ↓
Planning
 ↓
Mission
 ↓
Implementation
 ↓
Validation
 ↓
Knowledge Promotion
 ↓
Capability Improvement
 ↓
Organizational Evolution
```

The pipeline is cyclical rather than linear.

Organizational evolution generates better future ideas.

---

# Stage Definitions

## 1. Idea

Potential improvements, opportunities, or problems are identified.

Outputs:

* problem statement,
* business objective,
* initial assumptions.

---

## 2. Discovery

The organization develops a shared understanding of the problem.

Activities include:

* research,
* stakeholder analysis,
* constraint identification,
* success criteria.

---

## 3. Architecture

Permanent engineering decisions are documented.

Activities include:

* RFC creation,
* architectural review,
* design validation,
* technology evaluation.

Architecture precedes implementation.

---

## 4. Planning

The approved architecture is decomposed into executable Missions.

Planning defines:

* priorities,
* ownership,
* dependencies,
* milestones.

---

## 5. Mission

Professionals execute the planned work.

Outputs include:

* implementation,
* documentation,
* tests,
* operational assets.

Mission execution is coordinated by the Organization rather than individual contributors.

---

## 6. Implementation

Approved designs become working software.

Implementation should conform to:

* engineering standards,
* architectural boundaries,
* repository governance,
* documentation requirements.

Implementation does not redefine architecture.

---

## 7. Validation

Outputs are evaluated against defined objectives.

Validation includes:

* engineering review,
* testing,
* architectural verification,
* documentation review.

Only validated outputs may become permanent organizational assets.

---

## 8. Knowledge Promotion

Validated artifacts are promoted into permanent organizational knowledge.

Examples include:

* standards,
* blueprints,
* reusable patterns,
* engineering guidance,
* lessons learned.

Promotion is deliberate and governed.

---

## 9. Capability Improvement

Promoted knowledge strengthens organizational capabilities.

Examples:

* faster future delivery,
* improved engineering quality,
* expanded professional competencies,
* reusable organizational processes.

The organization becomes objectively stronger.

---

## 10. Organizational Evolution

The Organization updates itself through accumulated capability improvements.

Evolution may include:

* new standards,
* refined workflows,
* additional blueprints,
* expanded competencies,
* governance improvements.

This stage closes the engineering loop.

---

# Pipeline Characteristics

The Forge Pipeline is:

* repeatable,
* measurable,
* explainable,
* knowledge-driven,
* organization-centric,
* technology-independent.

Every execution should increase long-term organizational value.

---

# Governance

Each pipeline stage has explicit entry and exit criteria.

Transitions between stages should be deliberate and reviewable.

Major architectural changes require documented approval before implementation proceeds.

Knowledge promotion requires validation.

---

# Success Metrics

Pipeline success is measured using organizational outcomes rather than implementation volume.

Representative indicators include:

* promoted Knowledge Objects,
* reusable Blueprints,
* capability growth,
* reduced repeated work,
* engineering quality,
* decision traceability,
* onboarding efficiency.

Lines of code are not considered a primary success metric.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Traditional SDLC

Rejected because it focuses primarily on software delivery.

ForgeOS focuses on organizational capability.

---

## Agile Alone

Rejected because Agile defines planning and execution practices but not permanent organizational knowledge management.

ForgeOS incorporates iterative delivery while extending it with organizational learning.

---

## AI-Driven Autonomous Development

Rejected because autonomous implementation without governance weakens explainability, architectural integrity, and institutional knowledge.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* continuous organizational improvement,
* reusable engineering knowledge,
* consistent engineering quality,
* stronger governance,
* explainable architectural evolution,
* durable institutional memory.

Trade-offs include:

* additional governance,
* increased documentation,
* deliberate validation before promotion.

These trade-offs are accepted because they maximize long-term organizational capability.
