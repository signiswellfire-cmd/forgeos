# RFC-0003 — Knowledge Graph

**RFC Number:** RFC-0003

**Title:** Knowledge Graph

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Knowledge Graph architecture of ForgeOS.

The Knowledge Graph is the organizational memory system that connects every permanent knowledge object, engineering artifact, architectural decision, professional, mission, process, capability, and blueprint into a single navigable network.

Unlike traditional documentation systems that organize information hierarchically, the Knowledge Graph models relationships explicitly, enabling contextual reasoning, traceability, reuse, and organizational learning.

---

# Motivation

Engineering knowledge rarely exists in isolation.

A single engineering decision may relate to:

* multiple RFCs,
* several implementation artifacts,
* numerous completed missions,
* organizational standards,
* architectural principles,
* future engineering work.

Traditional folder structures preserve documents.

They do not preserve relationships.

ForgeOS therefore models relationships as first-class organizational assets.

---

# Problem Statement

Without explicit relationships:

* knowledge becomes fragmented,
* duplicated solutions emerge,
* engineering rationale becomes difficult to discover,
* impact analysis becomes unreliable,
* organizational learning slows over time.

Repositories preserve files.

Organizations require connected knowledge.

---

# Goals

The Knowledge Graph shall:

* connect permanent knowledge objects;
* support engineering traceability;
* enable contextual retrieval;
* preserve decision lineage;
* improve organizational learning;
* remain independent from implementation technology.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* graph database technology;
* storage engine;
* graph query language;
* embedding models;
* search algorithms;
* visualization.

Those concerns belong to future Technical Design Specifications.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the **Knowledge Graph** as the canonical relationship model for organizational knowledge.

Every permanent Knowledge Object may participate in one or more graph relationships.

Relationships themselves become permanent engineering knowledge.

---

# Graph Model

The Knowledge Graph consists of:

* Nodes
* Relationships
* Metadata
* Version History

Nodes represent knowledge.

Relationships explain meaning.

Metadata supports governance.

Version history preserves evolution.

---

# Node Types

The initial graph supports the following node categories.

## Organization

Represents the root organizational entity.

---

## Professional

Represents permanent organizational responsibilities.

---

## Team

Represents temporary execution structures.

---

## Mission

Represents organizational objectives.

---

## Process

Represents repeatable organizational workflows.

---

## Knowledge Object

Represents reusable organizational knowledge.

---

## Blueprint

Represents reusable organizational solutions.

---

## Capability

Represents organizational competencies.

---

## Decision

Represents permanent engineering decisions.

---

## Artifact

Represents outputs created during execution.

---

## Event

Represents meaningful organizational changes.

---

# Relationship Types

Relationships are directional unless explicitly defined otherwise.

Initial relationship types include:

## derives_from

Indicates inherited knowledge.

---

## references

Indicates informational dependency.

---

## depends_on

Represents implementation or conceptual dependency.

---

## validates

Represents formal verification.

---

## supersedes

Indicates replacement while preserving historical traceability.

---

## produces

Connects execution with resulting artifacts.

---

## promotes

Connects validated artifacts to permanent knowledge.

---

## implements

Connects implementation artifacts with architectural decisions.

---

## belongs_to

Represents organizational ownership.

---

## assigned_to

Represents temporary execution responsibility.

---

## uses

Represents capability utilization.

---

## enables

Represents capability creation.

---

# Graph Properties

Every relationship should preserve:

* source,
* destination,
* relationship type,
* creation timestamp,
* author,
* version,
* rationale.

Relationships should remain independently explainable.

---

# Traceability

Every permanent engineering decision should support bidirectional traceability.

Example:

```text
Mission
   ↓ produces
Artifact
   ↓ promoted_to
Knowledge Object
   ↓ references
RFC
   ↓ implemented_by
Source Code
```

An engineer should be able to navigate either direction through the graph.

---

# Knowledge Discovery

The Knowledge Graph supports:

* impact analysis,
* dependency discovery,
* organizational onboarding,
* engineering reasoning,
* architectural exploration,
* contextual AI assistance.

Knowledge retrieval should prioritize connected context over keyword matching.

---

# Graph Evolution

The graph evolves continuously.

New nodes and relationships may be added.

Existing relationships should rarely be deleted.

Historical relationships remain valuable organizational knowledge.

Where relationships change, supersession should be preferred over removal.

---

# Governance

Graph integrity is an architectural responsibility.

Relationship creation should preserve:

* correctness,
* consistency,
* traceability,
* explainability.

Invalid relationships weaken organizational reasoning.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Folder Hierarchies

Rejected because hierarchical storage cannot adequately model cross-cutting engineering relationships.

---

## Wiki Links

Rejected because hyperlinks lack semantic meaning and structured governance.

---

## AI Context Only

Rejected because contextual reasoning should remain independent of any AI implementation.

The Knowledge Graph is an organizational asset.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* connected organizational knowledge;
* explainable engineering decisions;
* improved architectural discovery;
* stronger AI context generation;
* reusable engineering knowledge;
* reliable impact analysis.

Trade-offs include:

* increased metadata management;
* relationship governance;
* graph validation requirements.

These trade-offs are accepted because they significantly improve long-term organizational capability.
