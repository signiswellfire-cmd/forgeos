# RFC-0009 — Knowledge Promotion

**RFC Number:** RFC-0009

**Title:** Knowledge Promotion

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Knowledge Promotion process within ForgeOS.

Knowledge Promotion is the mechanism through which temporary engineering outputs become permanent organizational assets.

The objective is to ensure that organizational capability grows continuously through deliberate validation rather than accidental accumulation.

Knowledge Promotion operationalizes the ForgeOS philosophy:

> **Every Mission should strengthen the Organization.**

---

# Motivation

Engineering organizations create large quantities of information.

Most of it is temporary.

Examples include:

* conversations,
* implementation notes,
* prototypes,
* experiments,
* issue discussions,
* pull requests.

Only a small subset deserves to become permanent organizational knowledge.

ForgeOS therefore distinguishes between **information** and **knowledge**.

---

# Problem Statement

Without a formal promotion process:

* valuable engineering experience is lost,
* duplicate solutions emerge,
* architectural decisions become inconsistent,
* onboarding becomes increasingly expensive,
* organizations repeatedly solve the same problems.

Permanent knowledge must be intentionally curated.

---

# Goals

Knowledge Promotion shall:

* identify reusable engineering knowledge;
* validate organizational value;
* preserve engineering rationale;
* strengthen organizational capability;
* improve future engineering work;
* remain independent from implementation technology.

---

# Non-Goals

Knowledge Promotion does not determine:

* engineering correctness,
* implementation quality,
* source code review,
* deployment approval.

Those responsibilities belong to their respective engineering processes.

Promotion evaluates long-term organizational value.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces a governed Knowledge Promotion lifecycle.

No engineering artifact automatically becomes organizational knowledge.

Promotion requires validation, review, and explicit organizational approval.

---

# Promotion Sources

Knowledge may originate from:

* completed Missions,
* Executive Meetings,
* architectural reviews,
* RFCs,
* TDSs,
* TDRs,
* implementation retrospectives,
* operational incidents,
* engineering experiments.

Any artifact demonstrating long-term organizational value may become a promotion candidate.

---

# Promotion Lifecycle

Knowledge Promotion follows the lifecycle below.

## Candidate

Potential knowledge identified.

Candidate status does not imply organizational approval.

---

## Evaluation

The candidate is evaluated for:

* accuracy,
* general applicability,
* architectural consistency,
* organizational value,
* long-term usefulness.

---

## Validation

Subject matter experts validate the engineering quality.

Validation confirms:

* correctness,
* completeness,
* explainability,
* consistency.

---

## Promotion

Approved knowledge becomes a permanent Knowledge Object.

The promoted object receives:

* identifier,
* ownership,
* lifecycle state,
* graph relationships,
* version history.

---

## Maintenance

Promoted knowledge evolves over time.

Future updates should preserve historical traceability.

---

## Retirement

Knowledge may eventually become obsolete.

Retirement does not remove knowledge.

Instead it transitions into a deprecated or archived state while preserving organizational history.

---

# Promotion Criteria

Knowledge should generally satisfy the following requirements.

## Reusable

Applicable beyond a single Mission.

---

## Explainable

Engineering reasoning is preserved.

---

## Validated

Reviewed by the appropriate organizational authority.

---

## Stable

Not dependent upon temporary implementation details.

---

## Valuable

Improves future organizational capability.

---

# Promotion Targets

Promoted knowledge may become:

* Knowledge Objects,
* Blueprints,
* Engineering Standards,
* Architecture Guidance,
* Organizational Processes,
* Capability Improvements,
* Executive Memory.

Promotion target depends upon the nature of the knowledge.

---

# Organizational Effects

Successful promotion should produce measurable organizational improvement.

Examples include:

* improved engineering quality,
* reduced implementation effort,
* fewer repeated mistakes,
* stronger onboarding,
* enhanced architectural consistency.

Knowledge Promotion is therefore considered an organizational investment rather than documentation activity.

---

# Governance

Promotion authority depends upon knowledge type.

Examples:

| Knowledge Type         | Typical Authority               |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Engineering Standard   | Chief Software Architect        |
| Organizational Process | Executive Leadership            |
| Blueprint              | Architecture Office             |
| Strategic Knowledge    | Founder or Executive Leadership |

Detailed authority rules are governed by the Decision Authority Matrix.

---

# Relationship to the Forge Pipeline

Knowledge Promotion occurs after Validation within the Forge Pipeline.

```text
Mission
    ↓
Artifacts
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Knowledge Promotion
    ↓
Capability Improvement
```

Promotion is the transition from execution to organizational learning.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Every promoted Knowledge Object becomes a node within the Knowledge Graph.

Promotion also establishes semantic relationships with:

* originating Missions,
* Decisions,
* Blueprints,
* Capabilities,
* Standards,
* Executive Memory.

Promotion therefore strengthens organizational connectivity as well as knowledge quality.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Automatic Promotion

Rejected because not every artifact has lasting organizational value.

---

## Manual Documentation Only

Rejected because documentation alone lacks lifecycle management and governance.

---

## AI-Generated Knowledge

Rejected because generated content must still undergo organizational validation before becoming permanent knowledge.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* continuously improving organizational capability;
* stronger institutional memory;
* reusable engineering knowledge;
* improved engineering consistency;
* reduced duplication of effort.

Trade-offs include:

* governance overhead,
* review effort,
* lifecycle management.

These trade-offs are accepted because they create durable organizational assets.
