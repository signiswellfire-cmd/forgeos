# RFC-0029 — Team Formation Engine

**RFC Number:** RFC-0029

**Title:** Team Formation Engine

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Team Formation Engine, the subsystem responsible for assembling, managing, evolving, and dissolving Mission Teams within ForgeOS.

Unlike conventional project management systems where teams are static organizational units, ForgeOS creates temporary, mission-oriented teams composed of Professionals selected according to organizational objectives, competencies, capabilities, authority, and workload.

Teams are execution constructs.

The Organization remains the permanent structure.

---

# Motivation

Traditional organizations often create permanent teams regardless of current organizational needs.

This frequently results in:

* uneven workload;
* duplicated expertise;
* capability gaps;
* organizational silos;
* inefficient collaboration.

ForgeOS instead forms teams dynamically around organizational Missions.

---

# Problem Statement

Without an explicit Team Formation model:

* Mission staffing becomes subjective;
* Professionals are underutilized;
* capabilities become unevenly distributed;
* organizational resilience decreases;
* execution quality varies.

ForgeOS requires deterministic, explainable team formation.

---

# Goals

The Team Formation Engine shall:

* assemble balanced Mission Teams;
* maximize capability coverage;
* optimize organizational collaboration;
* preserve governance;
* remain explainable;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

The Team Formation Engine does not define:

* HR organization charts;
* payroll structures;
* employment contracts;
* communication platforms;
* project scheduling algorithms.

These belong to separate organizational systems.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the Team Formation Engine as the authoritative subsystem responsible for creating Mission Teams.

Every Mission Team shall be assembled according to organizational requirements rather than fixed departmental structures.

Team formation is governed by organizational intelligence.

---

# Team Definition

A Team is a temporary organizational structure created to accomplish one or more Missions.

Each Team contains:

* Team Identifier;
* Mission Assignment;
* Organizational Objective;
* Team Lead;
* Participating Professionals;
* Required Competencies;
* Required Capabilities;
* Authority Boundaries;
* Collaboration Model;
* Lifecycle State;
* Performance Indicators.

Teams exist only while organizational objectives require them.

---

# Team Lifecycle

Every Team progresses through:

## Proposed

Mission staffing requirements identified.

---

## Designed

Candidate Professionals evaluated.

Capability coverage verified.

---

## Approved

Organizational governance authorizes team creation.

---

## Active

Team executes assigned Mission(s).

---

## Evaluated

Mission outcomes reviewed.

Knowledge captured.

---

## Dissolved

Team responsibilities conclude.

Organizational knowledge remains.

Professional identities remain unchanged.

---

# Team Formation Principles

## Mission Before Department

Teams are formed around Missions.

Departments do not constrain execution.

---

## Competency Balance

Teams should collectively satisfy Mission competency requirements.

No single Professional should become an irreplaceable dependency.

---

## Capability Coverage

Every required organizational Capability should be represented.

Coverage takes precedence over headcount.

---

## Authority Alignment

Decision authority should exist within the Team.

Approval responsibilities must remain clear.

---

## Knowledge Distribution

Knowledge should be shared across Professionals.

Mission execution should strengthen the Organization rather than isolated individuals.

---

# Team Composition

Representative Professional roles include:

* Executive Sponsor;
* Mission Lead;
* Chief Software Architect;
* Product Manager;
* Engineering Manager;
* Backend Engineer;
* Frontend Engineer;
* QA Engineer;
* DevOps Engineer;
* Technical Writer;
* Security Engineer.

Organizations may define additional Professional types.

---

# Team Selection

Professional selection considers:

* Competency Profile;
* Skill alignment;
* Capability contribution;
* Mission experience;
* workload;
* authority;
* organizational priorities.

Selection should optimize long-term organizational capability.

---

# Collaboration Model

Professionals collaborate through:

* Executive Meetings;
* Mission planning;
* Engineering Standards;
* organizational Processes;
* Knowledge Objects;
* Blueprints;
* Engineering Memory.

Collaboration remains governed rather than ad hoc.

---

# Team Evaluation

Evaluation considers:

* Mission outcomes;
* engineering quality;
* Knowledge Promotion;
* Blueprint reuse;
* capability growth;
* governance compliance;
* organizational learning.

Evaluation emphasizes organizational value rather than individual productivity.

---

# Relationship to Professionals

Professionals remain permanent.

Teams are temporary.

Professional identity, competency, and authority are independent of Team membership.

---

# Relationship to Missions

Each Team exists to execute one or more Missions.

Mission completion may dissolve the Team or initiate reformation.

Mission requirements drive team evolution.

---

# Relationship to the Competency Matrix

The Competency Matrix guides Team composition.

Balanced competency coverage should be preferred over duplicate specialization.

---

# Relationship to Organization Health

Organization Health evaluates:

* collaboration effectiveness;
* capability distribution;
* staffing resilience;
* Mission execution quality.

Team Formation directly influences organizational health.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Teams become graph nodes connected to:

* Missions;
* Professionals;
* Competencies;
* Capabilities;
* Executive Meetings;
* Knowledge Objects;
* Decisions.

Historical team composition remains permanently traceable.

---

# Governance

Team creation requires:

* Mission approval;
* competency verification;
* capability coverage validation;
* authority review.

Team modifications should remain traceable.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Permanent Functional Teams

Rejected because fixed structures reduce organizational adaptability.

---

## Self-Assembling AI Agents

Rejected because organizational governance must determine workforce composition.

---

## Manual Team Assignment

Rejected because deterministic organizational reasoning provides greater consistency and explainability.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* improved Mission execution;
* balanced workforce utilization;
* stronger organizational resilience;
* enhanced collaboration;
* measurable capability growth;
* explainable staffing decisions.

Trade-offs include:

* workforce planning complexity;
* governance overhead;
* competency maintenance.

These trade-offs are accepted because they improve long-term organizational effectiveness.
