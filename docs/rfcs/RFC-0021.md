# RFC-0021 — Mission Engine

**RFC Number:** RFC-0021

**Title:** Mission Engine

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Mission Engine, the execution orchestration subsystem of ForgeOS.

The Mission Engine transforms organizational intent into coordinated execution by planning, assigning, tracking, validating, and completing Missions while preserving governance, organizational knowledge, and engineering quality.

Unlike conventional task management systems, the Mission Engine exists to strengthen organizational capability rather than simply complete work.

---

# Motivation

Organizations execute strategy through work.

However, most software treats work as isolated tasks detached from organizational knowledge, architectural decisions, and capability development.

ForgeOS instead models work as Missions that permanently contribute to organizational evolution.

The Mission Engine operationalizes this philosophy.

---

# Problem Statement

Traditional work management systems exhibit several limitations:

* tasks become disconnected from strategic objectives;
* engineering rationale is lost;
* completed work rarely strengthens organizational knowledge;
* execution focuses on activity rather than capability growth;
* governance is inconsistently applied.

ForgeOS requires an execution engine centered on organizational learning.

---

# Goals

The Mission Engine shall:

* orchestrate Mission execution;
* preserve organizational governance;
* integrate with Executive Meetings;
* produce reusable organizational knowledge;
* support explainable execution;
* remain implementation independent.

---

# Non-Goals

The Mission Engine does not define:

* user interface workflows;
* scheduling algorithms;
* project management methodologies;
* notification systems;
* AI implementation details.

These concerns belong to implementation specifications.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the Mission Engine as the authoritative execution subsystem.

Every organizational objective requiring coordinated execution shall be represented as a Mission.

Mission execution shall follow the Forge Pipeline and contribute to organizational capability.

---

# Mission Definition

A Mission represents a bounded organizational objective with measurable outcomes.

Each Mission contains:

* identifier;
* objective;
* scope;
* priority;
* owner;
* participating Professionals;
* required Capabilities;
* dependencies;
* expected deliverables;
* success criteria;
* lifecycle state;
* produced artifacts.

A Mission is the smallest independently governable unit of execution.

---

# Mission Lifecycle

Every Mission progresses through the following lifecycle.

## Proposed

Mission identified.

No execution has begun.

---

## Planned

Objectives, dependencies, resources, and governance have been defined.

---

## Approved

Execution has been authorized through organizational governance.

---

## Active

Professionals execute the Mission.

Artifacts, experience, and knowledge are continuously generated.

---

## Validation

Outputs are evaluated against defined success criteria.

Engineering quality is verified.

---

## Knowledge Promotion

Reusable knowledge produced by the Mission is evaluated for promotion.

Mission completion is not considered final until knowledge has been assessed.

---

## Completed

Mission objectives have been achieved and governance obligations satisfied.

---

## Archived

Mission retained for historical traceability.

---

# Mission Composition

A Mission may contain:

* work packages;
* implementation activities;
* reviews;
* validation activities;
* documentation;
* operational tasks.

These execution details remain subordinate to the Mission.

The Mission remains the authoritative organizational unit.

---

# Mission Ownership

Every Mission shall have:

* exactly one Mission Owner;
* one or more participating Professionals;
* optional Executive oversight;
* explicit organizational authority.

Ownership provides accountability.

Execution may be collaborative.

---

# Mission Planning

Mission planning considers:

* strategic alignment;
* organizational priorities;
* required Capabilities;
* Blueprint availability;
* dependencies;
* organizational risk;
* estimated organizational value.

Planning optimizes organizational outcomes rather than maximizing task throughput.

---

# Mission Execution Principles

Execution follows these principles.

## Knowledge First

Professionals consult existing Knowledge before creating new solutions.

---

## Blueprint Reuse

Approved Blueprints should be reused whenever appropriate.

Reinvention requires justification.

---

## Continuous Experience Capture

Experience is recorded throughout execution.

Learning is continuous rather than retrospective-only.

---

## Governance Throughout

Governance remains active during execution.

Quality gates are distributed across the Mission lifecycle.

---

## Capability Growth

Every completed Mission should strengthen at least one organizational Capability.

Repeated execution without organizational improvement is considered inefficient.

---

# Relationship to Executive Meetings

Executive Meetings create, prioritize, redirect, or conclude Missions.

Strategic reasoning remains separate from operational execution.

---

# Relationship to the Forge Pipeline

The Mission Engine implements the execution stages of the Forge Pipeline.

Pipeline governance remains authoritative.

The Mission Engine operationalizes approved pipeline behavior.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Promotion

Mission outputs become candidates for Knowledge Promotion.

Examples include:

* reusable patterns;
* architectural guidance;
* Blueprints;
* organizational improvements;
* engineering standards.

Mission success is measured partly by organizational learning.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Each Mission becomes a Knowledge Graph node connected to:

* Professionals;
* Decisions;
* Capabilities;
* Blueprints;
* Knowledge Objects;
* Artifacts;
* Executive Meetings.

Mission history therefore becomes permanently navigable.

---

# Governance

Mission approval authority follows the Decision Authority Matrix.

Mission execution should remain explainable through:

* explicit objectives;
* documented decisions;
* traceable outputs;
* measurable outcomes.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Task Management Systems

Rejected because tasks do not adequately represent organizational objectives or capability development.

---

## Project-Centric Execution

Rejected because projects frequently become administrative constructs disconnected from organizational evolution.

---

## Autonomous AI Execution

Rejected because autonomous execution without governance weakens explainability and institutional learning.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* explainable execution;
* stronger governance;
* improved organizational learning;
* measurable capability growth;
* durable engineering knowledge.

Trade-offs include:

* richer execution modeling;
* governance overhead;
* lifecycle management.

These trade-offs are accepted because they maximize long-term organizational effectiveness.
