# RFC-0018 — Decision Traceability

**RFC Number:** RFC-0018

**Title:** Decision Traceability

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Decision Traceability framework of ForgeOS.

Decision Traceability establishes a permanent, explainable chain connecting strategic objectives, architectural decisions, implementation artifacts, operational outcomes, and organizational learning.

Every significant decision should be traceable from its origin to its long-term organizational impact.

Traceability is considered a core organizational capability rather than a documentation exercise.

---

# Motivation

Engineering organizations continually make decisions.

Over time, teams frequently lose the answers to questions such as:

* Why was this designed this way?
* What problem was being solved?
* Which alternatives were rejected?
* Which implementation satisfies this decision?
* Which later decisions replaced it?
* What organizational knowledge resulted?

Without traceability, organizations repeatedly re-evaluate previously solved problems.

---

# Problem Statement

Repositories preserve files.

Issue trackers preserve discussions.

Source code preserves implementation.

None of these independently preserve the complete lifecycle of engineering decisions.

ForgeOS therefore requires a unified traceability model spanning the entire engineering organization.

---

# Goals

Decision Traceability shall:

* preserve engineering rationale;
* support explainable architecture;
* connect strategic intent with implementation;
* improve impact analysis;
* strengthen organizational memory;
* remain technology independent.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* issue tracking,
* Git workflows,
* source control implementation,
* graph database technology,
* visualization tooling.

Those concerns belong to implementation specifications.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces **Decision Records** as permanently traceable organizational assets.

Every significant architectural, organizational, strategic, and technical decision shall participate in an explicit traceability network.

Decision history is preserved rather than rewritten.

---

# Decision Record

Each Decision Record contains:

* unique identifier,
* title,
* category,
* problem statement,
* decision,
* rationale,
* alternatives considered,
* consequences,
* approval authority,
* effective date,
* lifecycle state,
* related artifacts,
* related knowledge.

Decision Records remain immutable except through versioned supersession.

---

# Traceability Model

Every Decision Record participates in one or more relationships.

Representative relationships include:

* originates_from
* informed_by
* discussed_in
* approved_by
* implemented_by
* validated_by
* promoted_to
* superseded_by
* impacts
* references

Relationships are first-class organizational assets.

---

# Traceability Chain

A complete engineering trace should resemble:

```text
Vision
   ↓
Strategic Objective
   ↓
Executive Meeting
   ↓
Decision
   ↓
RFC
   ↓
TDS
   ↓
Implementation
   ↓
Validation
   ↓
Knowledge Promotion
   ↓
Capability Improvement
```

An engineer should be able to navigate the chain in either direction.

# Decision Lifecycle

Every Decision Record progresses through:

1. Proposed
2. Under Review
3. Approved
4. Implemented
5. Active
6. Superseded
7. Archived

Historical decisions remain traceable even after supersession.

---

# Traceability Requirements

Every approved decision should identify:

* originating problem,
* responsible authority,
* supporting knowledge,
* implementation artifacts,
* affected capabilities,
* successor decisions (if any).

No permanent decision should exist in isolation.

---

# Impact Analysis

Decision Traceability enables:

* dependency analysis,
* architectural impact assessment,
* organizational change planning,
* regression investigation,
* capability evolution,
* historical engineering research.

Impact analysis should rely upon documented relationships rather than individual memory.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Decision Traceability extends the Knowledge Graph.

Decision Records become graph nodes connected to:

* Missions,
* Professionals,
* Knowledge Objects,
* Blueprints,
* Capabilities,
* Standards,
* Artifacts.

Traceability therefore strengthens organizational reasoning.

---

# Governance

Major organizational decisions require:

* documented rationale,
* explicit approval,
* traceable relationships,
* lifecycle management.

Undocumented architectural decisions are considered organizational risk.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Git History Only

Rejected because commit history lacks organizational context and engineering rationale.

---

## Meeting Notes

Rejected because discussions do not establish permanent organizational decisions.

---

## Source Code References

Rejected because implementation cannot adequately explain strategic intent.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* explainable engineering history;
* improved architectural governance;
* stronger organizational memory;
* simplified impact analysis;
* better onboarding;
* reduced repeated analysis.

Trade-offs include:

* relationship maintenance,
* governance effort,
* additional metadata.

These trade-offs are accepted because they preserve long-term engineering knowledge.
