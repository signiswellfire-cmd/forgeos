# RFC-0011 — Capability Library

**RFC Number:** RFC-0011

**Title:** Capability Library

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Capability Library of ForgeOS.

The Capability Library is the authoritative repository of everything an Organization has demonstrated it can reliably accomplish. While the Blueprint Library preserves reusable solutions, the Capability Library records proven organizational competencies.

Capabilities represent organizational maturity rather than implementation features.

---

# Motivation

Engineering organizations often describe themselves by products, technologies, or team structures.

Those characteristics change over time.

The more durable measure of an engineering organization is its capabilities.

Examples include:

* delivering production software,
* performing architectural reviews,
* conducting security audits,
* operating local AI,
* managing engineering knowledge,
* executing complex migrations.

ForgeOS therefore models capabilities as first-class organizational assets.

---

# Problem Statement

Without an explicit capability model:

* organizational strengths are difficult to measure,
* mission assignment becomes inconsistent,
* professional growth lacks direction,
* engineering planning becomes subjective,
* AI cannot accurately reason about organizational competence.

Capabilities must therefore be modeled independently from people, projects, and technologies.

---

# Goals

The Capability Library shall:

* define organizational competencies;
* support mission planning;
* enable capability-based reasoning;
* measure organizational evolution;
* improve workforce composition;
* remain technology independent.

---

# Non-Goals

The Capability Library does not define:

* employee performance,
* implementation details,
* programming languages,
* project planning,
* organizational hierarchy.

These concerns belong to separate architectural domains.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS introduces the Capability Library as the authoritative catalog of validated organizational capabilities.

Capabilities are owned by the Organization and evolve through completed Missions, promoted Knowledge, and validated organizational experience.

Capabilities cannot be claimed—they must be earned through evidence.

---

# Capability Definition

A Capability represents a repeatable organizational ability to achieve a defined outcome with acceptable quality.

A Capability should be:

* measurable,
* explainable,
* reusable,
* evidence-based,
* continuously improvable.

Capabilities belong to the Organization rather than individual Professionals.

---

# Capability Structure

Each Capability contains:

* unique identifier,
* name,
* description,
* purpose,
* prerequisites,
* competency level,
* evidence,
* related Blueprints,
* related Knowledge Objects,
* related Missions,
* lifecycle state,
* version history.

# Capability Levels

Capabilities evolve through defined maturity levels.

## Level 1 — Emerging

Initial capability demonstrated.

---

## Level 2 — Repeatable

Capability consistently reproduced.

---

## Level 3 — Managed

Capability follows standardized organizational processes.

---

## Level 4 — Optimized

Capability is continuously measured and improved.

---

## Level 5 — Institutionalized

Capability is deeply integrated into organizational culture and engineering practice.

---

# Capability Acquisition

Capabilities develop through:

* successful Missions,
* Knowledge Promotion,
* Blueprint adoption,
* Professional development,
* organizational improvement initiatives.

Capability growth should be measurable.

---

# Capability Validation

Capabilities require objective evidence.

Examples include:

* completed Missions,
* engineering metrics,
* architectural reviews,
* production deployments,
* promoted Knowledge Objects.

Assertions without evidence do not establish capability.

---

# Relationship to Professionals

Professionals contribute to Capabilities.

Professionals do not own Capabilities.

This distinction ensures organizational competence survives personnel changes.

---

# Relationship to Blueprints

Blueprints accelerate capability acquisition.

Repeated Blueprint use should improve capability maturity while maintaining architectural consistency.

---

# Relationship to Missions

Mission planning should consider required Capabilities.

Mission outcomes may strengthen existing Capabilities or establish new ones.

Capability improvement is a measurable Mission outcome.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Capabilities are graph nodes connected to:

* Professionals,
* Missions,
* Blueprints,
* Knowledge Objects,
* Decisions,
* Processes.

These relationships enable organizational reasoning and capability discovery.

---

# Governance

Capability creation and advancement require validation.

Capability maturity should be periodically reviewed using objective organizational evidence.

Capabilities should never advance solely through subjective assessment.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Skill Matrix Only

Rejected because skills describe individuals rather than organizational competence.

---

## Project Experience

Rejected because completed projects do not necessarily establish repeatable organizational capability.

---

## Technology Certifications

Rejected because certifications alone do not demonstrate sustained engineering competence.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* measurable organizational growth;
* improved mission planning;
* capability-driven workforce development;
* stronger organizational resilience;
* explainable engineering maturity.

Trade-offs include:

* evidence collection,
* governance,
* periodic reassessment.

These trade-offs are accepted because they improve long-term organizational capability.
