# RFC-0043 — Multi-Organization Architecture

**RFC Number:** RFC-0043

**Title:** Multi-Organization Architecture

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Multi-Organization Architecture (MOA), the architectural model that enables ForgeOS to manage multiple independent Organizations within a single platform while preserving autonomy, governance, identity, security, and organizational sovereignty.

Rather than treating an enterprise as a single organizational entity, ForgeOS recognizes that modern enterprises frequently consist of multiple business units, subsidiaries, regional organizations, joint ventures, partner ecosystems, and customer organizations.

Each Organization operates independently while participating in a governed organizational ecosystem.

---

# Purpose

The Multi-Organization Architecture enables ForgeOS to:

* host multiple Organizations;
* preserve organizational sovereignty;
* enable governed collaboration;
* share knowledge through federation;
* support enterprise-wide governance;
* scale organizational intelligence across organizational boundaries.

The Organization becomes the primary architectural boundary of ForgeOS.

---

# Scope

This RFC defines:

* organizational hierarchy;
* organizational boundaries;
* cross-organization governance;
* organizational relationships;
* shared services;
* organizational isolation.

Implementation infrastructure is intentionally excluded.

---

# Context

Previous RFCs define the architecture of a single ForgeOS Organization.

This RFC extends that architecture to support multiple Organizations operating within a shared ForgeOS ecosystem.

Organizations may be:

* companies;
* subsidiaries;
* business units;
* government agencies;
* educational institutions;
* customer tenants;
* partner organizations.

Each Organization remains independently governed.

---

# Problem Statement

Traditional enterprise platforms frequently assume:

* one organization;
* one knowledge repository;
* one governance model;
* one identity;
* one operational structure.

These assumptions do not scale to enterprise ecosystems.

ForgeOS therefore requires Organization as a first-class architectural boundary.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS adopts the Organization as the highest operational boundary.

Every architectural entity belongs to exactly one Organization.

Cross-organization collaboration occurs only through governed federation mechanisms.

Organizational boundaries remain explicit throughout the platform.

---

# Architectural Principles

## Organization First

Every persistent entity belongs to an Organization.

No entity exists outside organizational ownership.

---

## Organizational Sovereignty

Organizations independently govern:

* knowledge;
* workforce;
* capabilities;
* governance;
* processes;
* strategic planning;
* organizational identity.

---

## Shared Platform

Organizations may share platform infrastructure while maintaining complete logical isolation.

Infrastructure sharing does not imply organizational sharing.

---

## Explicit Collaboration

Cross-organization collaboration shall always be intentional and governed.

Implicit organizational access is prohibited.

---

## Explainability

Every cross-organization relationship shall be traceable.

Organizational boundaries remain visible throughout execution.

---

# Organization Model

Every Organization contains:

* Organization Identifier;
* Organization DNA;
* Vision;
* Mission;
* Governance Model;
* Professionals;
* Teams;
* Capabilities;
* Knowledge Graph;
* Executive Memory;
* Engineering Memory;
* Organization Memory;
* Digital Twin;
* Organizational Intelligence;
* Strategic Plans.

Organizations remain autonomous operational units.

---

# Organizational Relationships

Representative relationships include:

## Parent Organization

Provides strategic oversight.

---

## Subsidiary Organization

Operates independently while inheriting selected organizational characteristics.

---

## Partner Organization

Collaborates through governed federation.

---

## Customer Organization

Consumes organizational capabilities while maintaining sovereignty.

---

## Community Organization

Participates in shared knowledge ecosystems.

---

# Organizational Isolation

Each Organization maintains independent:

* identity;
* governance;
* lifecycle;
* security policies;
* knowledge ownership;
* memory;
* strategic objectives;
* organizational health.

Isolation is logical rather than necessarily physical.

---

# Shared Services

ForgeOS may provide shared services including:

* identity infrastructure;
* execution platform;
* federation registry;
* marketplace;
* plugin ecosystem;
* monitoring;
* observability.

Shared services shall never compromise organizational autonomy.

---

# Cross-Organization Interaction

Organizations may interact through:

* Knowledge Federation;
* Blueprint exchange;
* Mission collaboration;
* shared capabilities;
* governed APIs;
* contractual relationships.

All interactions remain governed.

---

# Relationship to Knowledge Federation

Knowledge Federation defines how Organizations exchange knowledge.

The Multi-Organization Architecture defines the organizational boundaries within which federation occurs.

---

# Relationship to Organization Memory

Organization Memory remains private by default.

Organizations may explicitly publish selected institutional memories through governed federation.

---

# Relationship to Digital Twin

Each Organization owns exactly one Digital Twin.

Enterprise-level Digital Twins may aggregate multiple organizational twins while preserving organizational identity.

---

# Relationship to Organizational Intelligence

Organizational Intelligence remains organization-specific.

Enterprise Intelligence may aggregate insights from multiple Organizations without replacing individual organizational reasoning.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Each Organization owns an independent Knowledge Graph.

Federated graph relationships connect Organizations while preserving graph ownership.

The global graph is therefore a federation of governed organizational graphs rather than a single monolithic graph.

---

# Governance

Cross-organization governance requires:

* documented trust relationships;
* executive sponsorship;
* federation policies;
* ownership preservation;
* auditability;
* lifecycle governance.

Organizational autonomy shall not be compromised by enterprise convenience.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Single-Tenant Architecture

Rejected because it cannot represent enterprise organizational ecosystems.

---

## Shared Global Repository

Rejected because it weakens governance, ownership, and organizational sovereignty.

---

## Infrastructure-Based Isolation

Rejected because infrastructure boundaries should not define organizational architecture.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* enterprise scalability;
* organizational autonomy;
* secure collaboration;
* governed knowledge exchange;
* reusable platform infrastructure;
* explainable organizational relationships.

Trade-offs include:

* increased governance complexity;
* federation management;
* organizational metadata administration.

These trade-offs are accepted because they enable ForgeOS to scale from a single organization to enterprise ecosystems without sacrificing architectural integrity.
