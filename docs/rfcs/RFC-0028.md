**RFC Number:** RFC-0028

**Title:** Competency Matrix

**Status:** Approved

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Authors:** ForgeOS Architecture Office

**Last Updated:** 2026-08-01

---

# Abstract

This RFC defines the Competency Matrix, the organizational model used by ForgeOS to measure, visualize, govern, and evolve the competencies of Professionals and the Organization as a whole.

While the Skill Framework defines individual executable abilities and the Capability Library defines what an Organization can reliably accomplish, the Competency Matrix connects these concepts into a measurable organizational intelligence model.

The Competency Matrix becomes the authoritative mechanism for workforce planning, capability gap analysis, organizational maturity assessment, and long-term professional evolution.

---

# Motivation

Organizations frequently evaluate Professionals using subjective criteria.

Typical competency frameworks:

* differ between departments;
* are disconnected from engineering work;
* cannot support AI reasoning;
* become outdated;
* fail to represent organizational capability.

ForgeOS requires a competency model that is measurable, explainable, and directly connected to execution.

---

# Problem Statement

Without a Competency Matrix:

* capability gaps remain hidden;
* Mission staffing becomes inconsistent;
* succession planning becomes difficult;
* organizational maturity cannot be measured objectively;
* workforce development becomes reactive.

ForgeOS therefore requires a standardized competency model.

---

# Goals

The Competency Matrix shall:

* define organizational competencies;
* map Skills to Competencies;
* support Professional development;
* improve Mission staffing;
* identify organizational capability gaps;
* remain technology independent.

---

# Non-Goals

This RFC does not define:

* employee performance reviews;
* compensation;
* promotions;
* hiring policies;
* certification programs.

Those systems may consume the Competency Matrix but do not define it.

---

# Decision

ForgeOS adopts the Competency Matrix as the authoritative representation of professional competency throughout the Organization.

Every Professional is evaluated against a standardized set of Competencies.

Competencies are organizational assets rather than personal attributes.

---

# Competency Definition

A Competency represents a measurable domain of professional expertise composed of multiple related Skills.

Each Competency contains:

* Competency Identifier;
* Name;
* Description;
* Purpose;
* Associated Skills;
* Required Knowledge;
* Behavioral Expectations;
* Assessment Criteria;
* Maturity Levels;
* Related Capabilities;
* Version History.

# Competency Levels

ForgeOS defines six organizational competency levels.

## Level 0 — None

No demonstrated competency.

---

## Level 1 — Foundational

Basic understanding under supervision.

---

## Level 2 — Operational

Can perform routine organizational work independently.

---

## Level 3 — Advanced

Handles complex organizational responsibilities consistently.

---

## Level 4 — Expert

Recognized organizational authority.

Provides guidance and mentoring.

---

## Level 5 — Distinguished

Defines organizational direction.

Creates new engineering knowledge.

---

# Competency Assessment

Assessment should consider:

* demonstrated Mission outcomes;
* engineering quality;
* Knowledge contributions;
* Blueprint creation;
* organizational influence;
* peer validation;
* governance participation.

Assessment is evidence-based rather than opinion-based.

---

# Competency Development

Competencies improve through:

* Mission participation;
* Experience Capture;
* Knowledge Promotion;
* Blueprint development;
* mentoring;
* engineering practice.

Growth should be measurable across time.

---

# Competency Gap Analysis

The Competency Matrix enables identification of:

* missing organizational expertise;
* workforce imbalance;
* succession risks;
* training priorities;
* strategic investment opportunities.

Gap analysis supports executive planning.

---

# Relationship to Skills

Skills are atomic execution abilities.

Competencies organize related Skills into coherent professional domains.

A Competency may contain many Skills.

Each Skill belongs to one or more Competencies.

---

# Relationship to Capabilities

Capabilities emerge when sufficient organizational Competency exists across multiple Professionals.

Competency supports Capability.

Capability represents organizational outcomes.

---

# Relationship to Professionals

Each Professional maintains a Competency Profile.

Profiles evolve continuously through organizational work.

Competency Profiles support:

* Mission assignment;
* mentoring;
* organizational planning;
* workforce analytics.

---

# Relationship to Team Formation

The Team Formation Engine uses the Competency Matrix to assemble balanced teams.

Team composition should maximize:

* complementary competencies;
* capability coverage;
* organizational resilience.

---

# Relationship to the Knowledge Graph

Competencies become graph nodes connected to:

* Skills;
* Professionals;
* Missions;
* Capabilities;
* Blueprints;
* Engineering Memory.

These relationships enable explainable workforce reasoning.

---

# Governance

Competency definitions require architectural governance.

New Competencies should be introduced only when they represent durable organizational concepts.

Competency inflation should be avoided.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Department-Specific Competencies

Rejected because organization-wide reasoning requires a unified competency model.

---

## Technology-Centric Competencies

Rejected because technologies evolve more rapidly than professional expertise.

---

## Experience-Based Ranking

Rejected because years of experience do not necessarily indicate demonstrated competency.

---

# Consequences

Positive outcomes include:

* objective workforce planning;
* measurable professional growth;
* improved Mission staffing;
* capability gap visibility;
* stronger executive planning;
* explainable organizational intelligence.

Trade-offs include:

* competency governance;
* assessment effort;
* organizational calibration.

These trade-offs are accepted because they substantially improve workforce quality.
